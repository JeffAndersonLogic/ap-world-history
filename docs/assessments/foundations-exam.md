# Foundations Unit Exam: 40 Multiple Choice Questions

AP World History: Modern &middot; BeHistorical &middot; Foundations 0 through 5

Every item on this exam is drawn from content students have actually been given:
the six Foundations First & 10 readings (`scripts/lib/foundations-f10-content.js`)
and the five Foundations eBook chapters
(`scripts/lib/deep-reading-content/foundations-1.js` through `foundations-5.js`,
compiled as `ebook/foundations.html`). Nothing here tests a fact that is not in
one of those two places. The answer key cites the section for every item.

## Why the exam is built in three parts

The request was for a hybrid rather than a full national-exam-style paper, and
the three parts do different jobs.

**Part I, stimulus-based (20 items, 8 stimulus sets).** This is the national exam
format: a source, then two or three questions that require reading it rather than
recalling around it. Half the exam, because it is the half that transfers to May.

**Part II, traditional (12 items).** Standalone content questions. These exist
because a paper made entirely of stimulus sets punishes a student twice for one
misread source, and because some things worth knowing (the Han census, the two
mechanisms behind trans-Saharan trade) are content, not inference. They are
written above recall level: each one asks what a fact is evidence *for*.

**Part III, historical thinking skills (8 items).** The item type asked for
directly: a set of sentences, and the student decides which one actually performs
the move. These are not invented from scratch. Foundations 5, section 07, names
the signature failure of each of the five skills, and every distractor in this
part is one of those named failures:

| Skill | The move | Its signature failure |
|---|---|---|
| Contextualization | Link a condition to an outcome | An introduction posing as context |
| Causation | Trace a mechanism | A feature posing as a cause |
| Continuity and change | Measure against a baseline | Progress posing as change |
| Comparison | Two cases inside one named category | A parallel list posing as a comparison |
| Argumentation | A claim someone could dispute | A summary posing as an argument |

So a student who has read Foundations 5 has been taught the answer key to Part III
in advance, which is the point. The skill is nameable, and this part checks
whether it is.

## Administration

- **Time:** 55 minutes. That is the AP pace of about 1 minute 20 seconds per
  stimulus item with a little slack, and it is deliberately generous for Part III,
  where the options are long and reading them is the task.
- **No images required.** Every stimulus is text or a table, so the exam prints
  and photocopies without any figure to source. If you want a map item, the Module
  01 instructional maps in `assets/images/instructional-maps/` are the on-topic
  place to take one from.
- **Sources are labeled honestly.** Primary sources appear as paraphrases and say
  so, because a paraphrase presented as a quotation is the one thing a course
  about evidence cannot do. Secondary excerpts from the readings are quoted
  verbatim and attributed.
- **Suggested weighting if you scale:** Part I at 2 points, Parts II and III at 1
  point each gives a 60 point exam weighted toward source work. Straight 40 points
  works fine too.

> "Wild wheat and barley disperse their seed by shattering: when the grain ripens, the stalk holding it becomes brittle and the seeds fall to the ground, which is exactly what the plant needs and exactly what a harvester does not want. why the strongest distractor fails. The distractor notes are the part worth
projecting during review.

## Part I

**1. B** &middot; F1 eBook, section 07 &middot; Causation
The demographic scissors: fertility and mortality both rose, and fertility won. **A** inverts the central fact, since farming produced far more food per hectare, which is the whole reason it spread. **C** imports a later institution. **D** is not supported by anything in the evidence.

**2. A** &middot; F1 eBook, section 07, "How we know" &middot; Sourcing
Skeletal samples are a sample of the buried and the excavated, and elite burials preserve and get dug first. **B** and **C** are simply false. **D** is false and anachronistic. If students want extra credit, the osteological paradox is the deeper version of the same limitation: visible chronic disease means the person survived long enough to develop it.

**3. C** &middot; F1 eBook, section 07 &middot; Argumentation
The row exists so that "improvement for whom, measured by what, over what timescale" becomes a question the student has to answer. **B** reverses the point: the arrangement spread despite costs to the individuals paying them. **D** overstates in the opposite direction.

**4. B** &middot; F1 eBook, section 03 &middot; Causation
The whole passage is a mechanism running without intention. **A** is a real misconception, but it is section 04's, not this one's. **C** and **D** are not claims the passage engages.

**5. B** &middot; F1 eBook, section 03, "How we know a species was domesticated" &middot; Sourcing
The non-shattering scar on the grain is the direct physical trace of the process described. **A** is sedentism, which in the Levant preceded farming, so it proves the wrong thing. **C** is food processing, which foragers also did. **D** is a depiction of harvesting, which is the human behavior, not the change in the plant.

**6. B** &middot; F2 eBook, section 08 &middot; Continuity and change
Syncretism visible as town planning. **A** is contradicted by the king's own town and its sacred groves. **C** misreads a commercial quarter as segregation. **D** is contradicted by the twelve mosques.

**7. B** &middot; F2 eBook, section 08; F4 eBook, section 03 &middot; Causation
Islam supplied literacy, law, and access to a commercial world, which is why a non-Muslim king wanted Muslim officials. **A**, **C**, and **D** are inventions.

**8. A** &middot; F2 eBook, section 08, "How we know" &middot; Sourcing
Distance, not bias, is the first thing to say about al-Bakri. **B** reverses his position. **C** is false, since the archaeology at Koumbi Saleh bears on it. **D** is the overcorrection the reading explicitly warns against: griot tradition is Malian evidence with its own conventions and interests, not automatically better evidence.

**9. B** &middot; F3 eBook, section 01 &middot; Comparison of two sources
Cyrus does the local work and Darius does the cosmic work, and both are the same strategy at two altitudes. **A**, **C**, and **D** each describe a policy Persia did not follow.

**10. B** &middot; F3 eBook, section 01, "Common mistake to avoid" &middot; Argumentation
Tolerance was cheaper and more durable than terror, which is not the same as kindness. **A** is factually wrong. **C** and **D** describe an Achaemenid religious policy that did not exist.

**11. B** &middot; F3 eBook, section 01 &middot; Causation
Naming rebellion as a lie makes obedience into cosmic order and revolt into a category error, which is a stronger position than calling it a crime. **A** inverts the inscription's purpose.

**12. B** &middot; F3 eBook, section 03 &middot; Interpreting quantitative evidence
Roughly ten to twenty percent, which is the figure every reconstruction agrees on regardless of the exact numbers. **A** is contradicted by the arithmetic. **C** is false and backwards, since pay for public service existed precisely so poor citizens could serve. **D** is contradicted by the table, where enslaved people are the largest excluded group.

**13. A** &middot; F3 eBook, section 03 &middot; Argumentation
Pericles' law narrows membership at the peak of Athenian power, which is the evidence that democracy and exclusion grew together. **B**, **C**, and **D** are all broadenings of participation and therefore support the opposite reading.

**14. B** &middot; F4 eBook, section 02 &middot; Causation
The wind built the diaspora communities. **A** inverts the timescale. **C** is contradicted by the trade itself. **D** is nonsense but worth watching for.

**15. B** &middot; F4 eBook, section 02 &middot; Comparison
Predictability is what makes credit and forward planning possible. **A** is true of the Silk Roads, not the Indian Ocean, which is exactly the contrast: a ship carries timber, grain, and pottery that no caravan could justify. **C** is false of both networks. **D** is unconnected.

**16. C** &middot; F4 eBook, section 07 &middot; Continuity and change
Nobody adopts vocabulary for a single transaction. A language is the hardest evidence available that a network reshaped a society rather than visiting it. **A**, **B**, and **D** are all real and all commercial or military rather than consequences of seasonal residence.

**17. A** &middot; F4 eBook, section 05, "How we know" &middot; Sourcing
Assert what is solid, hedge what is single-sourced. **B** is the claim the passage is written to qualify: al-Tha'alibi wrote two and a half centuries later, and paper was in Central Asia before the battle. **C** and **D** contradict the passage outright.

**18. B** &middot; F4 eBook, section 05 &middot; Causation
A technology matters in proportion to how many other activities it makes cheaper, and paper is an input to administration, law, commerce, and scholarship at once. **A** reverses the point, since cheapness is the whole argument. **C** overstates by erasing manuscript culture on papyrus and parchment. **D** puts printing in the wrong place and the wrong century.

**19. B** &middot; F5 eBook, sections 01, 02, and 06 &middot; Comparison
Cities of a million against cities of tens of thousands, and Aristotle arriving in Latin through Iberia and Sicily. **A** is the error of projecting the ending onto the beginning. **C** is contradicted by the Dar al-Islam row. **D** is contradicted by both the Dar al-Islam and Latin Europe rows.

**20. C** &middot; F5 eBook, section 02, "Common mistake to avoid" &middot; Argumentation
Ibn Rushd and Maimonides are writing in the 1190s, and the madrasa network is expanding, centuries after the caliphs lost real power. Note the stem asks which claim the row argues **against**: A, B, and D are all claims the row supports.

## Part II

**21. B** &middot; F1 eBook, section 04 &middot; Causation
Different crops, no contact, near-simultaneous timing. A shared trigger acting on local materials is the only explanation that fits. **A** is the diffusion story the pattern rules out.

**22. B** &middot; F1 eBook, section 06 &middot; Causation
Storage is the hinge: it produces specialists, buffers bad years, permits accounting, and creates something worth taking. **A** is false, since early farming diets were narrower and often worse. **C** is false. **D** is a real consequence in some valleys but not the property being asked about.

**23. B** &middot; F1 eBook, section 06, "Common mistake to avoid" &middot; Argumentation
The Indus cities are the standing counter-case: write "surplus made hierarchy possible" rather than "surplus caused hierarchy."

**24. B** &middot; F2 eBook, sections 03 and 04 &middot; Comparison
Joined versus born into, which is the cleanest available explanation of divergent spread. **A** is false, since no Buddhist state conquered a region in order to convert it in this period. **C** is contradicted by the temple economy and the brahmin class.

**25. B** &middot; F2 eBook, section 03, "Common mistake to avoid" &middot; Causation
The guard against overcorrecting from item 24. Hindu traditions traveled as court religion and kingship ideology rather than as a membership open to converts. **A** is the overcorrection Angkor Wat makes indefensible.

**26. B** &middot; F3 eBook, section 02, "How we know" &middot; Sourcing
The number is evidence for the argument: a state that can count sixty million people household by household can tax and conscript them. **A** is a comparison the census cannot support on its own.

**27. B** &middot; F3 eBook, closing pairing, "legitimating authority" &middot; Comparison
The Mandate published the test it could fail, which is why the Yellow Turbans could rebel in the dynasty's own vocabulary. **A** misdescribes both. **D** is wrong on both halves, since the Mandate long predates the Han.

**28. B** &middot; F3 eBook, sections 01 and 04 &middot; Comparison
Tolerance without membership against membership itself. Persians themselves paid no regular tribute, and no conquered person could become Persian.

**29. A** &middot; F4 eBook, section 03 &middot; Causation
Two halves: camel physiology and saddle design made the crossing economical, and shared contract law, Arabic literacy, and credit instruments made a deal at one end enforceable at the other. **B** inverts who controlled the goldfields, which West African rulers deliberately kept obscure.

**30. B** &middot; F4 eBook, section 04 &middot; Causation
The mechanism is what transfers: rats live in stored grain, fleas live on rats, so a scheduled bulk food supply between dense cities is a scheduled pathogen delivery. **C** is the opposite of the reading's warning, since the mortality figures descend from Procopius and are genuinely disputed. Argue the mechanism, hedge the numbers.

**31. B** &middot; F5 eBook, section 05, "Common mistake to avoid" &middot; Contextualization
"Isolated from Afro-Eurasian networks," never "isolated." **A** runs isolation and backwardness together, which is the error the section exists to prevent. **D** is a chronology error worth its own review minute: the Aztec Triple Alliance forms in 1428 and Inca expansion begins around 1438, so neither exists at c. 1200, and Cahokia and Chaco are already past their peaks.

**32. A** &middot; F5 eBook, section 08 &middot; Periodization
There was no event in 1200. The boundary marks where the course starts looking. The student sentence is false in both directions at once, which is what makes it a good teaching example.

## Part III

The distractors here are the five named failure modes from F5 eBook, section 07.
Reviewing this part is faster if you name the failure rather than only the answer.

**33. C** &middot; Causation
**A** names a cause and stops. **B** is circular: the clause after "because" repeats the cause instead of explaining how it operated, which is the test the reading gives ("if the sentence after *because* repeats the cause, you have not finished"). **D** is description. Only C traces wind, to schedule, to seasonal residence, to diaspora.

**34. B** &middot; Contextualization
**A** and **D** are introductions posing as context: they name a background and connect nothing. **C** is description. B names conditions, 1127 and the wet-rice region, and says what they produced.

**35. C** &middot; Continuity and change
**A** overstates the change and loses the continuity. **B** is progress posing as change, the signature failure of this skill. **D** is narration with neither a continuity nor a change identified. C names both and gives the reason for the change.

**36. B** &middot; Comparison
**A** is the parallel list, accurate on both halves and comparing nothing. **C** asserts a similarity with no category and no evidence. **D** is a list of four tools with no category at all. B names the category first, which is what gives the rest of the sentence somewhere to go.

**37. B** &middot; Argumentation
**A** and **C** are unarguable. **D** announces a topic. B takes a position someone could reasonably dispute, supplies evidence on both sides, and lands on a qualified claim, which is the shape the reading recommends.

**38. B** &middot; Causation, failure identification
The Sahara's location is a feature. It was equally between West Africa and the Mediterranean for the millennia when the trade was marginal, which is exactly why a feature cannot be the cause. **A**, **C**, and **D** all supply mechanisms.

**39. C** &middot; Sourcing
A hostile source is read twice over: as evidence the practice existed, and as evidence of what the observer expected instead. **A** discards usable evidence. **B** ignores the author's position. **D** contradicts the source, since Ibn Battuta was at a Muslim court.

**40. C** &middot; Causation
**A** is the chain with the mechanisms removed and stated as a one-way line. **B** is environmental determinism, which the reading states fairly and then bounds. **D** is the automatic chain that New Guinea and the Indus cities both refute. C explains each junction and includes the counter-case, which is the sophisticated move available to a first-week student.

Randomizing the option order per item is the one preparation step this document
cannot do for you.

## Notes for review day

Three items are worth more than a minute each when you go over the paper.

**Items 24 and 25 together.** They are a matched pair. Item 24 rewards the
generalization that Buddhism traveled because membership was joinable, and item 25
punishes the overcorrection that Hindu traditions therefore did not travel at all.
A student who gets 24 right and 25 wrong has learned half a rule, which is the
most common outcome and the one worth naming out loud.

**Item 20.** The stem asks which claim the evidence argues **against**, and three
of the four options are claims the row supports. Students who miss it usually miss
the stem, not the history. It is a good place to teach reading the question.

**Part III as a whole.** The fastest review is to put option A, B, C, and D of one
item on the board and have students name the failure rather than pick the winner.
The vocabulary is already theirs: introduction posing as context, feature posing as
a cause, progress posing as change, parallel list posing as a comparison, summary
posing as an argument.

## What this exam does not cover

An honest list, so nobody assumes coverage that does not exist.

- **No Foundations 0 content questions.** The six skills are assessed by being
  performed in Part III rather than named, which is a defensible choice and is
  still a choice. If you want students to name the six skills, that is a separate
  item and it does not exist here.
- **No writing.** This is a multiple choice instrument only. The Foundations
  checkpoints and the First & 10 reflections remain the only place a student's
  argument in their own words reaches you.
- **Judaism is untested.** It is section 05 of the Foundations 2 chapter and one
  of the eight lecture cards, and no item touches it. The synagogue, the rabbi,
  and the text after 70 CE would make a strong portable-institution item paired
  against the waqf.
- **No map item.** Deliberate, so the exam prints without a figure, but Module 01
  work therefore goes unassessed.
- **No image or artifact item.** The Evidence Lab visuals across the six topics
  are not represented, which is the largest single gap if you want this exam to
  mirror what the modules actually ask students to do.

# Topic 4.6 Claim Ledger: Internal and External Challenges to State Power

**Status: started with the story draft, 2026-10-16; the "Where it appears" column is filled in as
each surface is written, and every row is re-checked in Phase 9.** Review findings and their
dispositions are at the end.

**The CED decides scope; scholarship decides truth.** The CED (`scripts/lib/ced-source/unit-4.js`,
p. 88) says what 4.6 must teach and names eight optional illustrative examples (Pueblo Revolts;
Fronde; Cossack revolts; Maratha conflict with Mughals; Ana Nzinga's resistance; Metacom's War;
Maroon societies in the Caribbean and Brazil; resistance of enslaved persons in North America). The
concrete cases inside them (Razin, Shivaji and Aurangzeb, Popé, Palmares, Cudjoe, Stono) are
teaching choices. Whether a sentence is true is settled by the sources recorded below, with what
they actually say. Where a source is an encyclopedia or a summary of the scholarship, the row says so.

Status is exactly one of VERIFIED, NARROWED, REMOVED, NEEDS JEFF.

## Sources leaned on, and in what role

- **Naranjo, Hackett and Shelby 1942:** "Declaration of Pedro Naranjo of the Queres Nation," Place of
  the Río del Norte, 19 December 1681, in Charles Wilson Hackett, ed., and Charmion Clair Shelby,
  trans., *Revolt of the Pueblo Indians of New Mexico and Otermín's Attempted Reconquest, 1680-1682*
  (Albuquerque: University of New Mexico Press, 1942), vol. 2, pp. 245 to 249. Read in the reprint on
  Teaching American History (Ashbrook Center), which cites Hackett and the American Journeys
  (Wisconsin Historical Society) scan of vol. 2, pp. 232 to 253. The CED's own sample activity (p. 77)
  names this document. Every quotation of Naranjo in the topic is checked against that text letter for
  letter. The translation is 1942; a short excerpt is quoted with full credit for classroom study (see
  the NEEDS JEFF row on its copyright status).
- **Smith, SC Encyclopedia:** Mark M. Smith, "Stono Rebellion," *South Carolina Encyclopedia*
  (University of South Carolina, Institute for Southern Studies). Smith is the editor of *Stono:
  Documenting and Interpreting a Southern Slave Revolt* (2005), the standard documentary collection.
- **De Waele, Europe 1450 to 1789:** Michel De Waele, "Fronde," *Europe, 1450 to 1789: Encyclopedia
  of the Early Modern World* (Scribner, 2004), on Encyclopedia.com. A specialist's entry.
- **Encyclopedia of Russian History:** "Razin, Stepan Timofeyevich" and "Law Code of 1649"
  (Macmillan Reference, 2004), on Encyclopedia.com.
- **Gomes, Encyclopedia of African-American Culture and History:** "Palmares" (Macmillan Reference,
  2nd ed., 2006), on Encyclopedia.com, with Funari and Price in its bibliography.
- **Dictionary of American History** and **Oxford Companion to American Military History:** "King
  Philip's War," on Encyclopedia.com.
- **Library of Congress, In Custodia Legis:** "1680: The Pueblo Revolt" (2013), with Otermín's letter
  of 8 September 1680. Its date of 21 September for the end of the siege is wrong (Otermín left Santa
  Fe on 21 August) and is not used.
- **National Park Service, Pecos:** "Spanish Encounters," Pecos National Historical Park.
- **Pritchett, Columbia:** Frances W. Pritchett (Columbia University), "Maratha power: its rise and
  fall," a teaching page with dated entries.
- **NCERT 2026:** *Exploring Society: India and Beyond*, Grade 8, Part 1, ch. 3, "The Rise of the
  Marathas" (National Council of Educational Research and Training, reprint 2026-27). Used for dates
  and the Ajnapatra quotation only; its framing is national and celebratory, and no interpretive claim
  rests on it alone.
- **Enslaved.org:** Nicolette M. Kostiw, adapted by Elizabeth Timbs and David Glovsky, "Njinga,"
  *Enslaved: Peoples of the Historical Slave Trade* (Michigan State University).
- **The 1739 articles:** "Articles of Pacification with the Maroons of Trelawney Town, Concluded March
  the first, 1738" [Old Style; 1739 New Style], as printed in the Journals of the Assembly of Jamaica
  and transcribed by the Berkman Center (Harvard), with the Assembly's act of 12 May 1739 confirming it.

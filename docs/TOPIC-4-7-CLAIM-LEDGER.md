**Status: started with the story draft, 2026-10-18; the "Where it appears" column is filled in as
each surface is written, and every row is re-checked in Phase 9.** Review findings and their
dispositions are at the end.

**The CED decides scope; scholarship decides truth.** The CED (`scripts/lib/ced-source/unit-4.js`,
p. 89) says what 4.7 must teach and names six optional illustrative examples (the expulsion of Jews
from Spain and Portugal and their acceptance in the Ottoman Empire; restrictive policies against Han
Chinese in Qing China; the varying status of different classes of women within the Ottoman Empire;
Ottoman timars; Russian boyars; European nobility). The concrete cases inside them (Bayezid II and
Salonica, Akbar and Aurangzeb, Hürrem and Kösem, the queue order, the banners, the 1695 tax farms,
the Table of Ranks, Versailles, England in 1688) are teaching choices. Whether a sentence is true is
settled by the sources recorded below, with what they actually say. Where a source is an
encyclopedia or a summary of the scholarship, the row says so.

## Sources leaned on, and in what role

- **Busbecq, Forster and Daniell 1881:** Charles Thornton Forster and F. H. Blackburne Daniell,
  eds. and trans., *The Life and Letters of Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq* (London: C. Kegan Paul,
  1881), vol. I, pp. 154 to 155, the First Turkish Letter, describing his audience with Suleiman at
  Amasya in 1555. Read in the archive.org scan of vol. I (identifier
  `busbecq-the-life-and-letters-of-ogier-ghiselin-de-busbecq-v-1`) and in the Fordham Internet Modern
  History Sourcebook's transcription, which prints the editor's name "Daniel." Public domain. Every
  quotation of Busbecq is checked against the 1881 scan letter for letter.
- **Montagu, Everyman 1906:** Lady Mary Wortley Montagu, letter "To the Countess of [Mar],
  Adrianople, April 1, O.S. [1717]," in *Letters* (Everyman's Library no. 69, London: J. M. Dent,
  1906), pp. 113 to 117, read in the archive.org scan (identifier `letters00mont`). The letters were
  first published in 1763, after her death. Public domain. Every quotation is checked against the
  scan letter for letter.
- **Wikipedia, for orientation and dates only, with the specialist work it cites:** "Expulsion of
  Jews from Spain"; "Table of Ranks"; "Boyar"; "Beard tax"; "Willow Palisade"; "Eight Banners";
  "Qing dynasty"; "Kösem Sultan"; "Haseki Sultan Complex"; "Casta painting" (citing Ilona Katzew,
  *Casta Painting*, Yale, 2004). Read on 2026-10-18. Where a claim is consequential or disputed it
  has a second source in its row.
- **Mazower, *Salonica, City of Ghosts* (2004), as summarized in reviews** (Cornucopia; eNotes; World
  History Connected): Jews the majority of Salonica's people from the 1500s, mainly Sephardim.
- **Jennings, Kayseri court records:** Ronald C. Jennings, "Women in Early 17th Century Ottoman
  Judicial Records: The Sharia Court of Anatolian Kayseri," *Journal of the Economic and Social
  History of the Orient* 18 (1975), reprinted in his *Studies on Ottoman Social History in the
  Sixteenth and Seventeenth Centuries* (Gorgias, 2011), as cited in later studies: women sued, were
  sued, and took part in about 40 percent of recorded property transactions in Kayseri.
- **Unit 3's own surfaces**, for agreement only (not as evidence): the 3.2 and 3.4 chapters on Akbar's
  1564 abolition and Aurangzeb's 1679 reimposition of the jizya, the Ottoman millet as a later
  formalization, and the move from timar cavalry to tax farming.

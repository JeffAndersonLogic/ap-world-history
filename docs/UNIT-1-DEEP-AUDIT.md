## Protected Unit 1 instructional spine

- **1.1:** Song governance + Chinese cultural/Buddhist influence + commercialization/innovation in the Song economy.
- **1.2:** effects of belief systems on society + rise/expansion of Islamic states + intellectual innovation/transfer.
- **1.3:** effects of Hinduism/Islam/Buddhism on society + how/why South and Southeast Asian states developed and maintained power.
- **1.4:** how/why American states developed/changed, using state-system evidence.
- **1.5:** how/why African states developed/changed, using Great Zimbabwe, Ethiopia, Hausa kingdoms and other appropriate evidence.
- **1.6:** effects of predominant religions on European society + causes/consequences of political decentralization + effects of agriculture/free and coerced labor on social organization.
- **1.7:** similarities/differences in **processes of state formation** across c. 1200–1450.

## Topic 1.1 — Developments in East Asia

### What is aligned

The runtime renderer correctly restores the Fall 2026 CED language for:
- Song Confucianism/bureaucracy;
- Chinese cultural traditions and Buddhist continuities/influence;
- Song commercialization and economic growth from productive capacity, trade, agriculture, and manufacturing.

The three learning targets broadly map to Learning Objectives A, B, and C.

### What needs repair

1. **The canonical base file still contains stale text for KC-3.1.I.D.**  
   The renderer corrects it at runtime, so students see the right framework language, but the base source still describes empire expansion facilitating trade rather than the current Song-economy development. That is source-of-truth debt and should be repaired rather than hidden by a renderer override.

2. **Checkpoint 2 claims to assess culture and economy but primarily asks an economic/trade-network question.**  
   Chinese cultural traditions, Buddhism, and influence on neighboring East Asian societies are relegated to a focus bullet rather than directly required by the prompt.

3. The explicit Indian Ocean/Silk Road connection in the checkpoint can remain contextual enrichment, but it should not replace Learning Objective B or turn 1.1 into an early Unit 2 trade lesson.

### Repair target

Use separate required moves:
**culture/tradition -> effect on neighboring East Asia** and **innovation/commercialization -> effect on Song economy**.

## Topic 1.2 — Developments in Dar al-Islam

### What is aligned

The three formal learning targets match the three Fall 2026 learning objectives:
- effects of belief systems/practices on society;
- causes/effects of the rise of Islamic states;
- effects of intellectual innovation.

Checkpoint 1 is a strong match for the political-state branch. Checkpoint 2 strongly assesses Islamic expansion networks and intellectual exchange.

### What needs repair

The first official objective is broader than the current checkpoint architecture. It asks how **Islam, Judaism, Christianity, and their practices continued to shape societies in Africa and Asia**. The current lesson/checkpoint treatment is dominated by Islam's spread and Islamic connectivity.

### Repair target

Require at least one formal assessment move on:
**belief/practice -> social effect**, not simply **agent/network -> spread of Islam**.

Keep Islam central to Dar al-Islam, but preserve the literal CED scope and distinguish social effects from religious diffusion.

## Topic 1.3 — Developments in South and Southeast Asia

### What is aligned

The canonical targets correctly include:
- Hinduism, Buddhism, and Islam shaping society;
- development/maintenance of state power.

The regional examples are strong and historically useful.

### What needs repair

1. **Learning Target 3 and Checkpoint 2 shift the formal assessment toward Indian Ocean trade.**  
   Indian Ocean exchange is important context and helps explain maritime state power, but it is not a separate Topic 1.3 learning objective in the Fall 2026 CED.

2. Checkpoint 2 asks how trade spread goods, religions, and cultural practices. Its subtitle claims to check the state-development objective, but the prompt does not actually require students to explain **how and why states developed and maintained power**.

3. Trade/geography should function as a causal explanation for Srivijaya/Majapahit or other states, not as the endpoint of the lesson.

### Repair target

Rewrite the second checkpoint around:
**geography/trade/religion/administration -> how a specific state developed or maintained power**.

## Topic 1.6 — Developments in Europe

### Priority finding

This is the largest Unit 1 mismatch.

The Fall 2026 CED gives Topic 1.6 three distinct learning objectives:
1. explain effects of predominant religions on European society;
2. explain causes and consequences of political decentralization;
3. explain effects of agriculture on European social organization, including free/coerced labor and serfdom.

The current BeHistorical target architecture instead uses:
- a generic target on European political structures, incorrectly labeled **Unit 1 Learning Objective H**;
- a combined target on Christianity, feudalism, manorialism, and cities;
- a Unit 1 comparison target.

### Assessment drift

- Checkpoint 1 addresses feudalism/nobles -> political fragmentation, which is useful but only part of the causes/consequences objective.
- Checkpoint 2 asks how Christianity unified Europe while towns/trade/universities showed change.
- No checkpoint directly asks students to explain **the effects of agriculture/free and coerced labor/serfdom on social organization**, despite this being Learning Objective M.
- The towns/guilds/universities material is useful context but is not one of the three governing historical developments for Topic 1.6.

### Repair target

Rebuild the formal target/checkpoint structure around:
**religion -> social effects**  
**decentralized political order -> causes and consequences**  
**agriculture + labor/serfdom -> social organization**

Comparison with Song China belongs in AP Skill Builder or 1.7, not as one of the three formal Topic 1.6 content targets.

## Topic 1.7 — Comparison

### What is aligned

The metadata includes the Unit 1 state-formation key concepts, and the first checkpoint correctly requires comparison of how two regions built/justified power.

### What needs tightening

The Fall 2026 Learning Objective N is specific:
**explain similarities and differences in the processes of state formation from c. 1200 to c. 1450.**

The current second target and second checkpoint broaden the comparison to belief systems, trade, and labor. Those are useful evidence/categories, but a student could currently produce a competent comparison paragraph that never actually compares **state formation**.

### Repair target

Every final comparison must explicitly answer:
**How were the processes by which these two states/regions formed, developed, or maintained political power similar and/or different?**

Belief, trade, labor, geography, and administration should function as explanatory evidence for state formation.

The current lack of a BeInTheRoom module is not a curricular defect. Topic 1.7 already has an integrated Teaching OS comparison experience; add a scenario only if it improves the state-formation reasoning.

## Repair order

1. **1.6** — restore the actual three-objective Europe spine.
2. **1.3** — make the second formal assessment state-development rather than trade diffusion.
3. **1.1** — fix stale base KC text and directly assess cultural influence.
4. **1.2** — make belief-system effects on society explicit.
5. **1.7** — constrain the capstone to state-formation comparison.
6. Preserve 1.4 and 1.5.

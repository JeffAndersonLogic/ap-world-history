## Sources leaned on, and in what role

- **Ligon 1657:** Richard Ligon, *A True & Exact History of the Island of Barbados* (London: Humphrey
  Moseley, 1657). Read in two transcriptions of the first edition: Project Gutenberg eBook #70489
  (2023), produced from the Internet Archive's scan, in the original spelling; and the EarlyPrint /
  EEBO-TCP text A48447, in modernized spelling (distantreader.org). Public domain. Every quotation is
  checked against the Gutenberg original-spelling text word for word; the page prints the spelling
  modernized and says so.
- **National Humanities Center, "Slavery on the English Island of Barbados"** (America in Class,
  2006), the introduction to its Ligon excerpts: Ligon "arrived on the island and purchased half of a
  functioning sugar plantation with several colleagues. He remained on the island for three years,
  writing *A True & Exact History* after his return to England."
- **Economic History Society, "The Barbados Census of 1679 and the Legacies of Slavery in Early Modern
  England"** (blog, the census compiled in December 1679 and sent to London in 1680): 37,700 enslaved
  African people. Wikipedia, "Afro-Barbadians," citing Dunn, *Sugar and Slaves* (1972): about 20,000
  white people in 1680.
- **Menard, *Sweet Negotiations* (2006), as summarized in its review** (Project MUSE): the servant
  population "rose in concert with the slave population until the 1650s"; he prefers "sugar boom" to
  "sugar revolution." Used to keep the Barbados story from saying servants were replaced at once.
- **The Unit 4 ledgers, `docs/TOPIC-4-1-CLAIM-LEDGER.md` to `docs/TOPIC-4-7-CLAIM-LEDGER.md`**, each
  row checked there against its own sources by three review passes. Cited by topic below.

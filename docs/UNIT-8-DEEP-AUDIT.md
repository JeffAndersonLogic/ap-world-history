## Protected Unit 8 instructional spine

- **8.1:** post-WWI unfulfilled self-government + post-WWII anti-imperialism + wartime gains shifting global power -> Cold War/decolonization context.
- **8.2:** U.S.–Soviet ideological struggle + alternatives such as the Non-Aligned Movement.
- **8.3:** compare U.S./Soviet methods of maintaining influence: alliances, nuclear proliferation, proxy wars.
- **8.4:** causes/consequences of Chinese communism **and** causes/effects of land/resource redistribution movements.
- **8.5:** compare independence processes: nationalist movements, negotiation, armed struggle, regional/religious/ethnic challenges.
- **8.6:** boundary redrawing -> new states/conflict/displacement **and** economic continuity/change after decolonization.
- **8.7:** nonviolent challenge, militarized-state escalation, and movements using violence against civilians.
- **8.8:** multiple interacting causes -> end of Cold War/Soviet collapse.
- **8.9:** evaluate extent of similarity in Cold War effects across Eastern/Western Hemispheres.

## Topic 8.1 — Setting the Stage

Targets preserve both required contextual strands:
- anti-imperialist sentiment after unfulfilled post-WWI hopes;
- wartime technological/economic gains shifting global balance.

The use of India/Ghana as contextual evidence is appropriate if kept as evidence of the transition rather than turning the topic into an early version of 8.5. Preserve.

## Topic 8.2 — The Cold War

Targets correctly cover:
- U.S.–Soviet ideological/power struggle;
- Non-Aligned alternatives.

The lesson also names NATO/Warsaw Pact, arms race, and proxy conflict as effects. This is defensible under the objective’s “causes and effects” wording, but those mechanisms are the explicit job of 8.3.

### Tightening target

Keep 8.2 centered on:
**why the ideological struggle emerged + how it divided the international order + why nonalignment offered an alternative.**

Use alliances/proxy wars as previews/effects, then let 8.3 own the comparative mechanism work.

## Topic 8.3 — Effects of the Cold War

Strong alignment to the comparison objective:
- NATO/Warsaw Pact;
- nuclear proliferation/deterrence;
- proxy wars in multiple postcolonial regions;
- compare methods of maintaining influence.

Preserve.

## Topic 8.4 — Spread of Communism After 1900

This topic correctly preserves both official objectives on the CED page:

1. causes/consequences of China’s adoption of communism:
   - internal tension + Japanese aggression -> communist seizure of power;
   - Great Leap Forward/repressive state economic control -> negative consequences.

2. causes/effects of movements to redistribute economic resources:
   - Vietnam,
   - Ethiopia,
   - Kerala/India,
   - White Revolution/Iran.

This was one of the outside calibration topics for the audit framework and behaves correctly as a dual-objective topic. Preserve.

## Topic 8.5 — Decolonization After 1900

Strong alignment:
- nationalist leaders/parties;
- negotiated vs. armed independence;
- regional/religious/ethnic movements challenging inherited boundaries.

The comparison question is explicit and useful. Preserve.

## Topic 8.6 — Newly Independent States

The current lesson correctly carries both official learning objectives G and H:

- political boundary change -> new states, conflict, displacement/resettlement;
- decolonization -> state-guided economic development + continued colony/metropole ties through migration.

This is a strong multi-objective build. Preserve, with source-sensitive handling of contested territorial/national narratives.

## Topic 8.7 — Global Resistance to Established Power Structures

Strong three-part structure:
- nonviolent political change;
- militarized/state responses that intensify conflict;
- movements using violence against civilians.

Preserve analytical distinctions between nonviolent resistance, state repression/militarization, and violence against civilians rather than collapsing all into “resistance.”

## Topic 8.8 — End of the Cold War

The target structure closely tracks the official three-factor causal statement:
- U.S. military/technological development;
- Soviet Afghanistan failure;
- public discontent/economic weakness.

### Precision issue

The lesson’s detailed SDI/Reagan discussion sometimes moves from “contributed to pressure” toward a stronger claim that U.S. policy deliberately forced an economically unsustainable Soviet response. That interpretation should remain explicitly **one causal analysis**, not a settled monocausal explanation.

Preserve the College Board multi-causal structure:
**external technological/military pressure + Afghanistan costs + internal economic weakness/public discontent**, with Gorbachev reforms as additional explanatory context.

## Topic 8.9 — Causation in the Age of the Cold War and Decolonization

The current build directly matches Learning Objective K:
**evaluate the extent to which Cold War effects were similar in the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.**

The cross-hemisphere evidence requirement and degree-claim thesis are appropriate. A BeInTheRoom module is not required for a synthesis/capstone topic. Preserve.

## Repair order

2. **8.2** — reduce duplication so 8.3 owns the compare-methods-of-influence work.
3. Preserve 8.1, 8.3–8.7, and 8.9.
4. Unit 8 is a strong candidate for locked CED/coherence contracts after these light revisions.

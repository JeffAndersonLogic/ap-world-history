## Protected Unit 7 instructional spine

- **7.1:** internal + external pressures -> collapse/change in states; Russian communist revolution; Mexican Revolution.
- **7.2:** imperial competition + regional conflict + alliance structure + nationalism -> World War I, with consequences explicitly bridged.
- **7.3:** total war mobilization + propaganda/media/nationalism + new military technology -> wartime casualties.
- **7.4:** postwar/Depression crisis -> stronger government intervention; compare intervention models; Soviet Five-Year Plans and human costs.
- **7.5:** continuity/change in imperial territorial holdings -> mandates/conquest alongside anti-imperial resistance.
- **7.6:** failed peace + Depression + imperial aspirations + fascist/totalitarian militarism -> World War II, with consequences explicitly bridged.
- **7.7:** compare democratic/totalitarian total-war mobilization + technology/tactics -> casualty escalation.
- **7.8:** causes and consequences of mass atrocities after 1900, using cases comparatively without forcing identical mechanisms.
- **7.9:** rank the relative significance of causes of global conflict.

## Topic 7.1 — Shifting Power After 1900

The current build directly protects the required developments:
- collapse of Ottoman, Russian, and Qing empires from combined internal/external pressures;
- Russian political crisis -> communist revolution;
- Mexican Revolution challenging political/social order.

Both checkpoints match the declared targets. Preserve.

## Topic 7.2 — Causes of World War I

The cause structure is exceptionally clear and aligned:
**imperialist competition + territorial/regional conflict + flawed alliance system + nationalism -> global war.**

### Precision issue

The current College Board Learning Objective B says **“Explain the causes and consequences of World War I,”** while the required historical-development statement on the same topic page is entirely cause-focused and the BeHistorical targets/checkpoints are cause-focused.

Do not rebuild the lesson around consequences. Instead add a concise bridge/exit move that names consequences students will immediately encounter in 7.5 and 7.1/7.4:
- collapse/redrawing of empires;
- territorial settlement/mandates;
- political/economic instability.

This closes the literal objective without diluting the topic’s cause-centered design.

Checkpoints directly assess both mobilization and technology/casualty mechanisms. Preserve.

The mandate system and Japanese expansion fit the official examples. Preserve.

## Topic 7.6 — Causes of World War II

The current lesson closely follows the official four-cause structure:
- unsustainable post-WWI peace;
- global economic crisis;
- continued imperialist aspirations;
- especially fascist/totalitarian regimes and aggressive militarism.

### Precision issue

As in 7.2, the College Board Learning Objective F says **“causes and consequences of World War II”** while the required historical-development statement emphasizes causes. The current lesson targets only causes.

Add a short bridge/exit that points forward to consequences already taught in 7.7–7.8 and later Unit 8:
- expanded total war/civilian casualties;
- mass atrocities;
- altered global balance and postwar order.

Do not turn 7.6 into a duplicate of 7.7 or Unit 8.

## Topic 7.7 — Conducting World War II

Strong comparative alignment:
- total-war mobilization in Western democracies and totalitarian states;
- ideology used to mobilize resources;
- repression/basic-freedom distinction;
- atomic bombing, fire-bombing, and total-war tactics -> casualties.

The current checkpoints preserve the official comparison objective and causation mechanism. Preserve.

## Topic 7.8 — Mass Atrocities After 1900

### Structural scan issue resolved

The course-wide structural scanner originally flagged 7.8 because its base lesson contains only the thematic-focus metadata. The renderer intentionally replaces that with the full Learning Objective H and KC-6.2.III.C at runtime. This is **not** a curricular failure; it is an architecture pattern the scanner should treat as informational once the runtime KC set is verified.

The lack of a BeInTheRoom scenario is also an **intentional safety/design decision**. The renderer substitutes a causes-and-consequences matrix so students analyze mass atrocities as historians rather than role-playing participants. Preserve that exception.

### Human-review issue

The lesson sometimes suggests that every illustrative case follows one identical sequence:
**extremist ideology in power -> dehumanization -> attempted destruction.**

That is a useful starting model for the Holocaust and some cases, but it is too uniform for all illustrative examples. The College Board framework groups distinct cases under mass atrocity/genocide/ethnic violence; it does not require asserting one identical causal mechanism for each.

Repair by:
- preserving case-specific political, wartime, ideological, ethnic, and institutional causes;
- avoiding language that turns a comparative pattern into a universal law;
- treating contested historical classifications with source-aware precision rather than collapsing all cases into one label.

The consequence checkpoint is well aligned to the objective’s “causes and consequences” requirement.

## Topic 7.9 — Causation in Global Conflict

This is a strong capstone. It correctly asks students to:
- identify causes;
- distinguish underlying causes from triggers;
- compare WWI/WWII;
- rank causes by **relative significance**;
- defend the ranking with specific Unit 7 evidence.

No BeInTheRoom is required simply because this is a synthesis topic. Preserve the causation matrix/capstone design.

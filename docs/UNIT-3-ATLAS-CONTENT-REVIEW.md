## Geographic and chronological rules

- City coordinates are rounded centers, not exact building or battlefield locations. Regional examples identify their scope in the label and panel. Chaldiran and the Gao/Tondibi discussion use regional markers rather than a claimed archaeological battlefield pin.
- Dates span the unit; markers are not a simultaneous political snapshot. Mexica and Inca examples refer to preconquest institutions in the early portion of this period. Ming revenue and later Qing portraiture remain distinct.
- No hand-drawn political polygons. Lines connect centers as schematic relationships, with no direction implying a verified march, road or campaign itinerary. Morocco–Gao is a rivalry relationship, not a surveyed Sahara crossing. European Reformation centers are not a single diffusion route.
- Lhasa marks an intervention in 1720, not simple uniform sovereignty across Tibet. Qing expansion after 1750 is excluded from map geometry.
- All student-facing factual copy is original concise explanation, not an invented primary-source quotation. The source disclosure is a bibliography and context panel.

## Protected Unit 5 instructional spine

- **5.1:** Enlightenment intellectual/ideological context **and** its effects on society over time, including reform/rights and feminism.
- **5.2:** causes/effects of revolutions and nationalism from 1750–1900.
- **5.3:** environmental and institutional factors that enabled early industrialization, including the factory system.
- **5.4:** changes in locations/modes of production as industrialization spread unevenly.
- **5.5:** fossil-fuel energy + second industrial revolution + rail/steamship/telegraph -> changed economic production.
- **5.6:** state-sponsored industrialization and the economic strategies of states/empires.
- **5.7:** free trade + transnational business/finance + industrial capitalism/consumer goods.
- **5.8:** reform responses, worker organization, socialism/communism, and modernization responses in Ottoman/Qing contexts.
- **5.9:** new classes + class-differentiated gender/family roles + urban problems.
- **5.10:** extent to which industrialization brought change from 1750–1900.

## Topic 5.1 — The Enlightenment

### Finding

The first target/checkpoint is strong on Enlightenment political ideas. The second target/checkpoint shifts toward **how ideas spread and the limits/hypocrisy of universalism**, which is valuable enrichment but does not directly protect the current Learning Objective B: the Enlightenment's effects on societies over time.

The official development branches include:
- reform movements contributing to expanded suffrage, abolition, and the end of serfdom;
- women's suffrage and emergent feminism challenging political/gender hierarchies.

### Repair target

Keep the spread/limits analysis, but make the formal second assessment answer:
**Enlightenment/religious ideals -> reform movements -> expanded rights / feminism**, using specific evidence.

## Topic 5.2 — Nationalism and Revolutions

The current build is fundamentally aligned:
- Enlightenment influence on Atlantic revolutions;
- Haitian/Latin American independence;
- nationalism as a new basis of political identity and state formation/unification.

The Haitian Revolution emphasis is enrichment rather than a replacement for the governing objective. Preserve the topic, while ensuring the broader American/French/Latin American and nationalism evidence remains visible.

## Topic 5.3 — Industrial Revolution Begins

The current targets align to the official topic:
- factors enabling industrial production;
- factory-system concentration and specialization.

The "Why Britain?" causal frame is useful, provided claims about any single cause remain qualified. Social effects can remain enrichment but should not steal assessment space from the governing environmental/institutional factors and factory-system mechanism.

## Topic 5.4 — Industrialization Spreads

This is one of the stronger builds. The targets correctly emphasize:
- spread to Europe, the United States, Russia, and Japan;
- declining Middle Eastern/Asian share of global manufacturing.

The colonial-policy/deindustrialization argument is legitimate contextual enrichment, but avoid reducing every non-industrialization outcome to a single deliberate-policy cause.

## Topic 5.5 — Technology of the Industrial Age

### What is aligned

The runtime metadata contains the correct required branches:
- fossil-fuel energy,
- second-industrial-revolution production,
- railroads/steamships/telegraph.

### What needs repair

1. Learning Target 1 is close to the governing objective.
2. **Learning Target 2 misuses KC-5.1.I.B.** It turns the fossil-fuel-energy development into a topic about environmental/social consequences and colonial resource extraction.
3. The corresponding checkpoint asks about rubber, guano, cotton, deforestation, and colonial labor systems. Those are useful consequences/imperialism links, but they do not assess the official 5.5 task: **how technology shaped economic production**.
4. Internal combustion/oil and the energy revolution need clearer formal assessment.

### Repair target

Assess:
**new energy source/machine -> production capacity -> transportation/communication -> trade/migration**, with consequences treated as enrichment.

## Topic 5.6 — Industrialization: Government's Role

### Priority finding

The current lesson is fundamentally assigned to the wrong topic job.

The current College Board Learning Objective G asks students to explain causes/effects of the **economic strategies of different states and empires**, with:
- state-sponsored industrialization;
- Meiji reform supporting Japanese industrialization/regional power.

The current BeHistorical 5.6 instead teaches:
- bourgeoisie/proletariat,
- liberalism/conservatism/socialism/Marxism,
- government social reform,
- women's roles/suffrage.

Those are mostly 5.8 and 5.9 concerns.

Even though the CED metadata includes the correct state-industrialization KCs, the actual targets, lecture, and checkpoints do not use them.

### Repair target

Rebuild around comparative state strategy:
**Why governments intervened -> what they did -> results/limits**, using cases such as Muhammad Ali's Egypt and Meiji Japan.

## Topic 5.7 — Economic Developments and Innovations

### What is aligned

- finance/transnational businesses;
- free trade / laissez-faire.

### What needs repair

1. The official topic also requires the effects of industrial capitalism on standards of living and consumer-goods availability.
2. The second target/checkpoint shifts heavily into the **gold standard and economic imperialism/debt dependency**. This is useful later-period enrichment but does not replace the required consumer-capitalism branch.
3. The topic should distinguish official "transnational businesses using banking/finance" from a much broader informal-empire argument.

### Repair target

Formal assessment should cover:
**free trade ideology -> transnational business/finance -> industrial capitalism -> changing consumer-goods availability/standards of living**.

## Topic 5.8 — Reactions to the Industrial Economy

### What is aligned

- worker organization and unions;
- political/social/educational/urban reforms.

### What is missing or displaced

The official topic also contains two major required branches:
- socialism/communism/Marx as ideological reactions;
- Ottoman and Qing efforts to reform/modernize in response to expanding industrial powers, including elite resistance.

The current second target/checkpoint instead emphasizes abolitionism, public education, public health, and temperance. Some are valid reform evidence, but they cannot replace Marx/socialism or Ottoman/Qing modernization.

### Repair target

Use four branches:
**worker organization -> reform movements -> socialist/communist alternatives -> non-Western state modernization/reform**.

## Topic 5.9 — Society and the Industrial Age

This topic is well aligned to the current CED:
- middle and working classes;
- different effects on working-class versus middle-class women/children;
- urbanization problems.

Preserve this as the social-consequences topic. Material currently sitting in 5.6 should be moved here only when it directly supports these required developments.

## Topic 5.10 — Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age

The current "extent to which industrialization brought change" frame matches Learning Objective K. The unit-wide argumentation structure is appropriate.

The main review task is to ensure the evidence students use comes from repaired 5.1–5.9 topic spines after those changes are made.

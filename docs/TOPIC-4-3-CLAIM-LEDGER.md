## Sources leaned on, and in what role

- **Nunn and Qian 2010:** Nathan Nunn and Nancy Qian, "The Columbian Exchange: A History of
  Disease, Food, and Ideas," *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 24:2 (2010), pp. 163 to 188. A
  scholarly survey; read in full (author copy, Kellogg School of Management). Used for disease
  lists, population estimates, crops and sugar, each with the scholarship it cites.
- **Jones 2003:** David S. Jones, "Virgin Soils Revisited," *William and Mary Quarterly* 60:4
  (2003), pp. 703 to 742. Read in full. The specialist source on why the epidemics were so deadly.
- **Florentine Codex, Book 12, ch. 29:** Bernardino de Sahagún and Nahua informants, in James
  Lockhart's translation of both columns, Early Nahuatl Library (Wired Humanities Projects,
  ed. Stephanie Wood), `enl.wired-humanities.org/FCBk12Ch29`. A primary source.
- **Acosta 1590:** José de Acosta, *The Natural and Moral History of the Indies*, Book IV, ch. 33,
  in Edward Grimston's 1604 translation, ed. C. R. Markham (Hakluyt Society, 1880), vol. 1,
  pp. 270 to 272 (archive.org, `naturalmoralhist60acosrich`). A primary source, public domain.

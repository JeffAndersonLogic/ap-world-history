# Course-Wide Module 05 Scaffolding Audit

**Scope:** Foundations 0–5 plus AP Units 1–9 — 77 topic pages total.

**Audit question:** Can a student independently identify the skill, execute its reasoning moves, access or select appropriate evidence at the level expected for this point in the course, and produce an AP-authentic response without the teacher having to restate the assignment orally?

See `docs/module-05-scaffolding-standard.md` for the full rubric and progressive-release rules.

## Executive finding

The course did **not** need a wholesale rewrite of Module 05. The later units already show a useful progression toward independent AP reasoning. The main weakness was concentrated in early Units 1–2, where several skill builders named a correct skill but required students to make too many invisible decisions at once: selecting a category, retrieving evidence, distinguishing context from argument, or explaining the mechanism between cause and effect.

The refinement therefore follows this rule:

- **Foundations + Units 1–2:** high support;
- **Units 3–5:** moderate support;
- **Units 6–9:** increasing independence.

## Foundations — 6 modules audited

**Expected level:** high support / skill introduction.

**Result:** no revisions required.

- **F0 Intro to BeHistorical** introduces the historical-thinking vocabulary through short, low-risk practice reps.
- **F1 Geography** explicitly teaches contextualization through development → geographic condition → causal connection → larger context.
- **F2 Belief Systems** teaches comparison through named cases and analytical categories. The existing skill-alignment report's F2 wording warning is a known false positive rather than a reason to relabel the module.
- **F3 States & Power** compares states through shared governance problems and requires mechanism, not parallel description.
- **F4 Trade Networks** introduces complexity by requiring multiple effects across regions and treating the network as a system.
- **F5 World at c.1200** synthesizes the Foundations work into an AP-style response and explicitly names the reasoning moves students have already practiced.

These modules are already more scaffolded than the regular-unit pattern and appropriately prepare students for Unit 1.

## Unit 3 — 4 modules audited

**Expected level:** moderate support.

**Result:** no revisions required.

The modules appropriately shift away from sentence frames and evidence menus while retaining the reasoning architecture. Across the unit, students practice cause → mechanism → effect, shared-problem comparison, evidence-based argumentation, and comparison tied to a larger historical claim.

## Unit 4 — 8 modules audited

**Expected level:** moderate support.

**Result:** no revisions required.

The skill builders already ask students to work with multi-factor causation, causal chains, empire-model comparison, economic causation, resistance, social hierarchy, and CCOT synthesis. The modules supply enough structure to make the reasoning visible without doing the evidence organization for students.

## Unit 5 — 10 modules audited

**Expected level:** moderate support moving toward independent argumentation.

**Result:** no revisions required.

The unit already contains strong examples of multi-factor causation, comparison, evidence → mechanism → significance, reform-ideology comparison, social change/continuity, and extent-of-change argumentation. Students are increasingly expected to select and organize evidence rather than choose from early-course menus.

## Unit 6 — 8 modules audited

**Expected level:** transition to independent AP reasoning.

**Result:** no revisions required.

Topic 6.1 already separates cause from justification and requires qualification. Topics 6.2–6.8 are generated with the deliberate pattern:

**claim → evidence mechanism → qualification**

That is exactly the support level wanted at this point in the course. Adding Unit 1-style matrices or sentence frames would work against the progression.

## Unit 7 — 9 modules audited

**Expected level:** high independence.

**Result:** no revisions required.

The unit requires students to distinguish internal/external causes, rank long-term/intermediate/immediate causes, source documents, compare government responses, track shifts in state power, weigh causes of World War II, compare conduct of war, trace atrocity mechanisms, and build a significance argument across multiple topics.

The scaffolding is primarily organizational rather than answer-generating, which is appropriate.

## Unit 8 — 9 modules audited

**Expected level:** high independence.

**Result:** no revisions required.

The unit asks students to generate context, compare superpower methods, classify Cold War and decolonization cases, track postcolonial continuity/change, defend evidence classifications, and rank causes by relative significance. Topic 8.9 functions as a capstone argument task rather than a guided early-course exercise.

## Unit 9 — 9 modules audited

**Expected level:** exam-ready independence.

**Result:** no revisions required.

Early topics ask students to trace technology, disease, and environmental causal chains with minimal support. Topics 9.4–9.9 use the generated late-course pattern:

**claim → evidence mechanism → qualification**

The evidence is named by the lesson, but the student must select, organize, weigh, and qualify it. That is the intended endpoint of the progressive release.

## Final course progression

The audited sequence now behaves as one skill-development system rather than 77 isolated Module 05s:

**Foundations:** learn what the moves are → **Units 1–2:** execute them with strong scaffolds → **Units 3–5:** make more evidence and organizational decisions → **Units 6–9:** build and qualify arguments with increasing independence.

Future Module 05 revisions should preserve this progression. The goal is not identical module design; the goal is the right amount of support for where students are in the year.

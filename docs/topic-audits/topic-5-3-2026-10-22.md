## Fixed
- CED: the story, the targets and both checkpoints answer learning objective D (how environmental
  factors contributed to industrialization, 1750 to 1900) with both Key Concepts: all seven of
  KC-5.1.I.A's factors, sorted into what nature gave (waterways, with canals as nature's water dug by
  people; coal, iron and timber) and what people built (towns, better farming, laws that protected
  property, foreign resources, capital), and KC-5.1.I.C's factory system (one place, specialized
  labor). The CED's Causation reasoning process and its 1.B skill; every Key Concept is banded on a
  projected slide. The CED prints no illustrative examples for 5.3; every case is the build's choice.
- First & 10 (required): rewritten from the story ("Coal, Water, and One Roof," seven sections). From
  review: the Bridgewater Canal opened in 1761 to Stretford and reached Manchester by 1765 (acts of
  1759 and 1760); coke iron spread slowly after Darby's 1709 start; Russian iron came later than
  Swedish; the farming share is a range of estimates, not one figure; the 1624 law banned most
  monopolies but kept patents, "for up to fourteen years"; "most" of Manchester's cotton was grown by
  enslaved people by the 1780s, never "all"; Smith quoted word for word.
- Skill Builder (required): 1.B, modeled on a bus line (a neutral example), so no model answer can be
  copied into a checkpoint.
- Checkpoint 1: one factor nature gave and one people built, with the mechanism; canals sit in the
  nature step, and the Bridgewater Canal is excluded as the student's own pair.
- Checkpoint 2 (required): how the factory changed the way cotton thread was made (one place; one step
  each), declared Causation; step 5 says "the fast water that turned Cromford's wheel," not coal.
- Evidence Lab (required): six cards, four real objects (Coalbrookdale by Night, 1801; the cotton
  plant after Merian, 1807; Allom's mule-spinning room, 1834; power-loom weaving, 1835) and two
  verified excerpts (Smith's pin workshop; section 6 of the 1624 statute), credits only as the
  holders' records state.
- Primary Source: Smith (two passages, the "without any of them having been educated" clause
  restored) and Ure (1835), with a "Words to know" gloss.
- BeSurreal: Cromford, 1772, a girl carding by hand before the carding machines.
- BeInTheRoom ("The Mill Partners," Nottingham, 1771): replaces "The Factory Commission" (1833), which
  belonged to 5.8 and 5.9. Students as Arkwright's partners decide where to build, how to divide the
  work, and how to protect and pay for it; the reflection asks whether a choice "does or does not
  show" a factory; the patent evidence carries the 1785 ruling in plain words.
- Map: rebuilt as a world map of Britain and the places it reached (the Caribbean and South America,
  then the US South, for cotton; Sweden and later Russia for iron), the key printing labels only,
  never the map question's answer.
- Teaching OS deck: 25 slides from the story, with the equation as the retelling slide; real pictures
  on the question and landing slides; slide 9's staircase and slide 21 fit in the brand fonts; slide
  17's Smith quotation with full ellipses; the carding plate credited to Thomas Allom's c. 1834
  drawing from the Science Museum Group's record of the original.
- Deep reading: rebuilt around the story (coal, water, food, risk, factory); its "use this in your
  answer" lines and closing card give the move and the evidence to sort, not finished answers; the
  glossary's canal reaches Manchester by 1765.
- Canvas text (staged, not generated until dates exist): the `OVERVIEWS` 5.3 entry.

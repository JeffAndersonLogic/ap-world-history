# Topic 3.1 audit, 2026-10-02

Taught: Green 2026-10-09, Silver 2026-10-19 (fall break falls between them). Neither
cohort has had it.

Audited the same day as the `/build-topic 3.1` build. The class presentation, the First &
10 reading, lecture cards 1 to 4 and BeSurreal were written in that build and fact-checked
there, so this pass concentrated on the surfaces the build did not write: the checkpoints,
the Evidence Lab, the Primary Source, the Map module and the BeInTheRoom scenario.

## Fixed

- **Checkpoint 2** (`lesson-3-1-renderer-config.js`): "explain ONE interstate rivalry
  named by the CED" is now "choose ONE rivalry between states ... and explain what caused
  it: how did a political or religious dispute lead to the conflict?" Same demand, plain
  words. It also clears the skill-alignment flag: the checkpoint is labeled Causation and
  its wording carried no causal language.
- **Evidence Lab task** (`lesson-3-1-renderer-config.js`): it told students "a map or
  portrait" cannot prove a mechanism, and there is no portrait card on the page (the cards
  are the Dardanelles Gun, the Panipat painting, the 1453 siege record, and maps of the
  Mughal and Safavid empires). Rewritten in plain language; "early modern", "political
  memory" and "causal mechanism" are gone.
- **Map module and lecture card 1** (`lesson-3-1-empires-expand.js`): both captions dated
  the animated Ottoman map "1359 to 1839". The file runs from 1300 to 1920 and ends on the
  Republic of Turkey, so students were watching the empire's fall under a caption about
  its rise. Both captions now say what the map shows and point students at the frames up to
  about 1700.

## Needs Jeff's decision

- **Primary Source, Module 08 (optional for 3.1).** It is labeled "an adapted passage from
  the account of the Ottoman historian Tursun Beg", but its wording cannot be matched to a
  published translation from here, and lines such as "where the great towers had stood for
  a thousand years, there were now heaps of stone" read as modern composition. The hijri
  date (857) and the "two seas and two continents" title are right, which makes it harder,
  not easier, to tell what is quoted and what is invented. **Recommendation:** replace it
  with an excerpt checked against the book (Tursun Beg in the Inalcik and Murphey
  translation, or Kritovoulos on the cannons in the Riggs translation), or relabel it
  honestly as a summary. Not changed, because I am not certain it is wrong and the fix
  needs the text in hand.
- **The rewritten First & 10 needs your approval of the prose.** `readings-golden.js`
  fails, by design, until the Unit 3 approval fingerprint is updated after you read it.

## Adjacent findings

1. **Instructional map drift.** `scripts/build-instructional-maps.js` rewrites
   `assets/images/instructional-maps/topic-2-6-crops.svg` differently from the committed
   file, which carries callout styles the spec does not produce. Impact: the next person to
   rebuild the maps silently reverts the 2.6 crop map. Proposed fix: move the callouts into
   the spec or the renderer, then add a `--check` mode to the offline suite. Left out
   because it is a 2.6 file; the 3.1 build kept its own map and reverted the 2.6 change.
2. **Module art drift.** `scripts/build-module-art.js` rewrites 578 files course-wide and
   adds missing `evidence-NN` fallbacks for most topics: the committed art lags the lesson
   data and nothing checks it. Proposed fix: rebuild once in its own commit and add a
   `--check` mode to the offline suite. Left out because it is course-wide; the 3.1 build
   kept only the three 3.1 files its own edits changed.
3. **Two generators have no `--check`.** `build-skills-map.js` and
   `generate-status-manifest.js` ignore the flag and rewrite their output, so "checking"
   them silently rebuilds. The status manifest change was a timestamp only and was
   reverted. Proposed fix: a `--check` that fails on drift without writing. Left out:
   tooling outside 3.1.
4. **Fixed in the build because 3.1 needed it, recorded because it is shared code.** The
   annotated slide template (`assets/js/behistorical-slide-templates.js`) drew its pins in a
   fixed SVG that drifted off the picture whenever the heading wrapped. 3.1 is the first
   deck to use it. The pins are now HTML on the same percentages as the frame, and
   `slide-templates.browser.test.js` checks every real annotated pin against its data, with
   a control.
5. **Fixed in the build, shared test.** `teaching-os-deck-overflow.test.js` counted a
   picture cropped by its own template frame (the route map zooming into a satellite image)
   as painted off the board. It now measures an image inside `.bht-frame` or `.bht-ro-map`
   by what the frame shows; text is still measured whole. Two controls prove a loose
   picture off the board is still caught and a framed one is not.

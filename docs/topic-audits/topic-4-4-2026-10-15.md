# Topic 4.4 audit, 2026-10-15

Taught: Green Thursday 2026-11-05, Silver Friday 2026-11-06. Neither room has had 4.4. This
audit closes the 4.4 build on `claude/topic-4-4-build`. The build began on 2026-10-14 in one
session, which ran out of usage partway through review pass 2; a second session took it over on
2026-10-15, reran pass 2 from scratch and ran pass 3. Three independent review passes, six
reviewers, plus a 42-claim fact-check of the build surfaces; every finding and its disposition
is in `docs/TOPIC-4-4-CLAIM-LEDGER.md` under "Review findings". The loop stopped on its
three-pass safety valve. This record covers the audit's own read after pass 3 and summarizes the
loop.

## Fixed

The fixes most likely to matter in class (full list in the ledger):

- **Answers no longer projected or published.** Slide 20 is a sentence frame instead of a model
  answer (every 4.4 case is asked for somewhere); the deep reading's closing cards list evidence
  instead of verdicts; the lecture card and the deep reading give the facts about Afonso's letter
  without the conclusion First & 10 Q2 asks students to reach.
- **Quotations credited to the translations they are.** Afonso's letter is Luís Madureira's
  translation (in John K. Thornton's 2023 book); the 1635 and 1639 Tokugawa orders are quoted
  verbatim from David J. Lu (1997), which two of the old wordings matched and two did not.
- **Pictures captioned as what they are.** The Elmina engraving is the Dutch-era plate with
  Coenraadsburg; Heydt never went to Japan; the crucifix caption places its figures where they are on the object; every
  on-screen credit says where a European picture was made; every Evidence Lab card has a
  descriptive alt text (the unit renderer now reads an optional `alt`).
- **Dates and overstatements.** "From the late 1400s" (Elmina is 1482); the Dutch the only
  Europeans in Japan from 1639, on Dejima from 1641; Nagasaki the main port for Portuguese and
  Chinese ships; the Swahili coast no longer listed as keeping its own rules; Mamluk Egypt, not
  the Ottomans, as Portugal's rival at sea in the 1515 scenario; Hormuz's tribute from 1507; the
  Caribbean majority narrowed to the British, French and Dutch colonies.
- **Page truth.** The Skill Builder asks for what the page gives (point of view and purpose of
  the 1639 order); Checkpoint 2 asks only what its criteria ask; the Evidence Lab gains an Indian
  Ocean card (Braun and Hogenberg's Mombasa, 1572) in place of a crucifix card whose inference
  was 4.5's religious blending.
- **Readability.** Bracketed glosses in both translated sources; the Goa scenario's role, option
  and evidence text rewritten in plain words, and "Estado da Índia" explained.

## Adjacent findings

- **Japan is drawn badly on every world map** (`scripts/lib/map-frame.js`).
  - The coarse East Asian coastline runs through the `japan` zone, and the Japan polygon is a
    thin sliver, so Japan reads as a stretch of mainland coast.
  - Impact: every map that shows East Asia (the `japan` zone is used in seven specs). On 4.4 it
    matters most, because Japan is one of the map's "places with their own rules".
  - Proposed fix: redraw Kyushu, Shikoku and Honshu as polygons and pull the Korea and Manchuria
    coastline west, then rebuild all maps and review every map that changes.
  - Left out because it changes maps across the course.
- **Map type is too small to read when projected** (`scripts/lib/map-render.js`, all 56 maps;
  first reported in 4.4's pass 1).
- **KC codes are uppercased by CSS** ("KC-4.3.II.A.I") on target chips and Key Concept bands
  across the course, while the CED and the data use lowercase roman numerals.
- **The frame-subtitle template's eyebrow** sits on the picture with only a fading scrim; on a
  light picture it measured 2:1 to 3:1. 4.4 drops its two eyebrows; the template is shared.

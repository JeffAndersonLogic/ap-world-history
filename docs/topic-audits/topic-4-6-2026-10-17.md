# Topic 4.6 audit, 2026-10-17

Taught: Green Wednesday 2026-11-11, Silver Thursday 2026-11-12. Neither room has had 4.6. Required
modules on both days: 02 First & 10, 05 Skill Builder, 07 Evidence Lab, 10 Checkpoint 2.

This audit closes the 4.6 build on `claude/build-4-6-a8qfom`. Phases 1 to 8 were built on
2026-10-16; Phase 9 ran on 2026-10-17. Three independent review passes, six reviewers (page truth
and readability on the cheaper model; history on the default model), each pass working from a
fresh packet from `capture-topic-for-review.js 4.6`: Chromium text captures of every module, the
reading, the chapter, the scenario and the Canvas text, and screenshots of all 28 teacher slides.
Every finding and its disposition is in `docs/TOPIC-4-6-CLAIM-LEDGER.md`, under "Review findings";
this record lists the outcome. The loop ended on the three-pass safety valve: 17, 12 and 7
supported defects, falling in size as well as number, and no pass found a CED alignment or
story-map ownership defect.

## Fixed
- First & 10 (required): the Common Mistake callout no longer prints Question 3's answer; the
  knotted cord follows Naranjo (a town that agreed untied one knot; the knots left counted the
  days); the dance ban dated 1661, not "the 1650s"; Stono's dead given as a range (between twenty
  and forty white colonists and dozens of rebels) with survivors hunted for weeks and months.
- Skill Builder (required): the Stono practice no longer says the rebels were caught the same day.
- Checkpoint 1: asks what the resistance changed, as its displayed target says; Success Criterion
  1 adds the comparison it asks for.
- Checkpoint 2 (required): Success Criterion 2 adds the reason the responses differed, which the
  prompt asks for.
- Evidence Lab (required): the Razin card credited to a book published in Leiden, not
  "made in Amsterdam"; Broussel, Shivaji and Aurangzeb captions name their unnamed makers, since
  the prompt asks who made each; the Broussel print no longer placed in Paris; the Shivaji prompt
  asks how the painter chose to show him; the Shivaji caption limited to the museum record; the
  Jamaica treaty credited to Dallas's printing of 1803, which its words match.
- Primary Source: the intro no longer answers Question 2 ("sorcerers"); Question 2 asks students
  to judge one phrase; Naranjo spoke Spanish, with interpreters present for the oath.
- Lecture cards: the missing `lecture-04.svg` generated (and `evidence-05`, `evidence-06`);
  "political" causes of the Pueblo Revolt are Spanish rule by force and raids not stopped, with
  drought and famine an extra pressure; Stono no longer "crushed in a day"; Broussel re-cropped
  from the museum file so the whole inscription and "Avec privilège 1648" show.
- Map: title c. 1600 to 1739 (it had contradicted its own Njinga and Palmares labels); Maratha
  war 1660s to 1707 on map, key and slide 9; the key lists places and dates, so the question's
  "who pushed against whom" is the student's to name.
- Deep reading: comparison sentences that gave checkpoint answers turned into questions;
  "That is what the College Board means" removed; the Razin overclaim narrowed; the Persian
  princess story attributed to Struys (1676); Palmares's 1678 treaty accepted by Ganga Zumba and
  rejected by Zumbi.
- Deck: slide 17's quotation credited to Dallas, with both printings in the notes; slide 16's
  "What it proves" is a question; slides 3, 7, 28 and notes credit the Luyken prints to their Leiden
  books; slide 9 notes give Aurangzeb's death near Ahmednagar; slide 10's Njinga date 1624 to 1663;
  slide 11 notes repaired; slide 18 Stono wording.
- BeInTheRoom: every role, evidence card, decision and the reflection prompt rewritten for a ninth
  grader; the knotted cord follows Naranjo; one tradeoff no longer misdescribes Pueblo rule.

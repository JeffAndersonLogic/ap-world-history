# Units 4 and 5 atlas content review

Reviewed before implementation on October 12, 2026 (UTC). Scope: selected geographic reference views for all 18 topics, not replacement lessons or assessments.

## Authority and checks

Content starts with the uploaded CED MASTER v1.1 Working Copy and AP World Curriculum Master 5.1; the College Board CED is the controlling framework. Historical checks use the sources linked in each topic panel: College Board; OpenStax World History Volume 2; Library of Congress; National Park Service; HSBC institutional history. Each marker describes a selected place, regional center, or dated event. Connections are explicitly schematic, not borders, uniform trade volumes, or exact itineraries.

Unit 4 coverage: cross-cultural navigation; state-sponsored oceanic travel; biological exchange and unequal demographic effects; European footholds and continuing Asian trade; coercive and contractual labor; silver, company finance, and regional manufacturing; state resistance; changing elites and colonial hierarchies; continuity and change.

Unit 5 coverage: reason, rights, and reforms; Atlantic independence and nationalism; Britain's industrial conditions and factories; uneven industrial spread; fossil fuels, steel, electricity, chemicals, and transport; state initiatives; markets and finance; workers, socialism, and state reform; gender, class, and urban conditions; continuity and change.

## Corrections and exclusions

- Columbus reached inhabited Caribbean societies in 1492, not an empty continent and not Asia. Earlier Norse contact means he was not the first European in the Americas.
- Portuguese ships entered existing Indian Ocean networks. They did not create them or replace all Asian merchants.
- Tokugawa restrictions were selective, not total isolation. Do not put the Qing Canton System (1757) inside Unit 4's c. 1450–1750 window.
- Atlantic forced migration is not one mandatory triangular itinerary. Brazil and Caribbean destinations matter; African regions and impacts varied. Do not give whole-trade lifetime estimates as 1450–1750 totals.
- Colonial mita adapted an earlier Inca institution. Encomienda is a tribute/labor grant, not simply land ownership; hacienda is an estate, not an identical labor system.
- Plantation crops and disease moved through multiple paths; biological exchange is not a single port-to-port voyage. Smallpox, war, exploitation, and disrupted communities combined in Indigenous demographic catastrophe; no universal percentage is asserted.
- Haiti's revolution began in 1791; independence was in 1804. Toussaint Louverture did not proclaim independence: Dessalines did.
- The United States declared independence in 1776; Britain recognized it in 1783. France's 1789 revolution did not create instant universal equality.
- Italy's unification in 1861 was incomplete; Rome joined in 1870. German unification was proclaimed in 1871. These markers do not draw national borders.
- Japan's Meiji Restoration dates to 1868; later industrial investment was a process, not instant transformation. Egypt's Muhammad Ali industrial projects belong to the early nineteenth century.
- Declining Asian and Middle Eastern share of manufacturing does not mean manufacturing vanished. No region uniformly industrialized.
- Railways and telegraphs are dated examples, not a completed global system in 1750. The Trans-Siberian railway was unfinished in 1900 and is not drawn as complete. No 1914 Panama Canal.
- The working copy's Unilever example is chronologically unsafe if presented as an existing 1750–1900 company: Unilever’s merger agreement dates to 1929 and its official establishment to 1930. Use HSBC (founded 1865) rather than repeat that ambiguity.
- Middle-class domestic ideals were not the experience of every woman. Working-class women and children earned wages; living standards and reform varied.

## Confidence standard

The requested 95% certainty is treated as a high-confidence source-review target, not a measured probability or a promise of 95% coverage. Use conservative, bounded claims; omit disputed precision. Technical tests separately check topic membership, geographic frames, source links, lesson destinations, assignment exclusion, generated-data drift, and responsive controls. Negative controls must prove new checks detect faults before green results are trusted.

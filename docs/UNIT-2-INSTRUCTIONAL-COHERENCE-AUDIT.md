# Unit 2 Instructional Coherence Audit

**Scope:** AP World History: Modern, Unit 2 — Networks of Exchange, Topics 2.1–2.7  
**Governing source:** `CED - MASTER v.1.1 - Working Copy.pdf`  
**Audit type:** Second-pass ecosystem audit after the canonical Unit 2 CED contract was repaired.  
**Important distinction:** The canonical lesson-data contract now covers 100% of the locked CED requirements, but this second audit asks whether every downstream student-facing surface reinforces the same topic story instead of reviving stale or adjacent content.

## Standard used

A surface does **not** need to repeat every CED fact. It does need to do one of four things correctly:

1. Build background for the topic's actual learning objective.
2. Teach or reinforce one part of the locked CED spine.
3. Assess the learning targets that the topic actually declares.
4. Enrich the topic without displacing required content or importing another topic's core work.

The audit reviewed the canonical learning targets/success criteria plus the following lesson surfaces where present: Map, First & 10, Content Delivery, BeSurreal, AP Skill Builder, Checkpoint 1, Evidence Lab, Primary Source, BeInTheRoom, Checkpoint 2, and Deep Reading/eBook content.

The percentages below are **manual instructional-coherence estimates**, not the automated canonical CED-contract score.

## Topic 2.1 — The Silk Roads

### Locked CED spine

**Luxury demand + transportation/commercial systems + lower risk -> expanded trade volume/range -> trading-city growth + expanded production.**

Required anchors include caravanserai, forms of credit, bills of exchange, banking houses, paper money, Kashgar, Samarkand, textiles, porcelain, and Chinese iron/steel production.

### What is aligned

- Canonical learning targets and success criteria.
- Rebuilt Content Delivery/Teaching OS.
- Primary-source framing around paper money/commercial systems.
- Evidence Lab is largely usable.
- BeInTheRoom's caravanserai/merchant scenario is strong: students make decisions about credit, commercial relationships, protection, taxation, and risk.
- Deep Reading is strong on caravan costs, caravanserai, credit, commercial risk, political stability, and trading nodes.

### Residual drift

- **First & 10:** still frames the topic as infrastructure + pastoral nomads + Pax Mongolica followed by technology/religion/plague diffusion. Its reading target and two of its four sections are largely later-Unit-2 content.
- **Map:** still elevates pastoral nomads as a core causal pillar rather than centering commercial mechanisms and nodes.
- **BeSurreal:** crop/food diffusion belongs primarily in Topic 2.6.
- **Skill Builder:** includes religions, technologies, disease, and cultural diffusion as central effects, restoring the old broad-survey frame.
- **Checkpoint 1:** asks about state support/pastoral nomads/caravanserai but does not require the credit/money-economy mechanisms or luxury demand.
- **Checkpoint 2:** is substantially misaligned, asking about goods, religions, technology, plague, and Black Death rather than cities and productive response.

### Required repair

Rewrite First & 10, Skill Builder, Checkpoint 1, and Checkpoint 2 around the locked economic chain. Reframe Map labels around trade nodes/commercial risk. Replace BeSurreal with a commercial-system or production story. Preserve BeInTheRoom and most of the Deep Reading.

## Topic 2.2 — The Mongol Empire

### Locked CED spine

**Build -> fragment/decline -> connect -> transfer.**

Students must understand Mongol state building/decline, expansion's effect on trade and communication, and the significance of technological/cultural transfers. Required examples include Greco-Islamic medical knowledge to western Europe, numbering systems to Europe, and adoption of the Uyghur script.

### What is aligned

- Canonical targets and rebuilt Content Delivery/Teaching OS.
- Khanates, governance, Yam, Pax Mongolica, trade, and fragmentation are well represented across the ecosystem.
- Deep Reading is excellent on conquest, administration, khanates, exchange, and collapse.
- BeInTheRoom strongly reinforces governance, local intermediaries, commerce, and life under Mongol rule.

### Residual drift

- **First & 10:** conquest/governance/Pax/destruction/plague dominate. The required cultural/technological transfer examples are absent.
- **Skill Builder:** uses generic diffusion and disease rather than the CED-selected transfer examples.
- **Checkpoint 2:** asks students to balance trade/diplomacy with plague; it does not assess the required transfer development.
- **Evidence Lab:** reinforces empire/trade geography but not the required transfer examples.
- **Deep Reading/eBook:** rich transfer material exists (astronomy, medicine between Persia/China, gunpowder, porcelain, specialists), but the specific CED examples — Greco-Islamic medicine to western Europe, numbering systems to Europe, Uyghur script — are not explicit enough.

### Required repair

Retain the excellent governance/Pax architecture, but add the three required transfer anchors to First & 10, Deep Reading/eBook, Evidence Lab, and an assessment. Reduce plague from a major 2.2 assessment strand to a bridge toward Topic 2.6.

## Topic 2.3 — Exchange in the Indian Ocean

### Locked CED spine

**Monsoon knowledge + maritime technology -> expanded trade -> stronger trading cities/states + diasporic communities + wider cultural/technological transfer.**

Required anchors include compass, astrolabe, larger ship designs, Swahili Coast city-states, Gujarat, Sultanate of Malacca, Arab/Persian communities in East Africa, Chinese communities in Southeast Asia, Malay communities in the Indian Ocean basin, Zheng He, and monsoon winds.

### What is aligned

- Canonical targets and Content Delivery.
- Map and BeInTheRoom strongly reinforce monsoon knowledge, ports, diasporic communities, and commercial strategy.
- Deep Reading/eBook is excellent: ship technologies, compass/astrolabe, port competition, diasporas, and a full Zheng He section are present.

### Residual drift

- **First & 10:** emphasizes monsoon/lateen sail, goods, diaspora, and Islam. It does not adequately teach astrolabe, larger ship designs, Zheng He, or state growth as a formal CED development.
- **Checkpoint 1:** maritime technology is assessed but the required astrolabe/larger-ship evidence is not expected.
- **Checkpoint 2:** still uses the older goods/diaspora/Islam frame and does not assess state growth or Zheng He.
- **Evidence Lab:** strong for environment/technology/connectivity but weak for state growth and Zheng He.

### Required repair

Bring First & 10 and both checkpoints into the same four-part structure as the rebuilt core: environment, maritime technology, states/diasporas, Zheng He. Add at least one state-growth or Zheng He evidence card.

## Topic 2.4 — Trans-Saharan Trade

### Locked CED spine

**Camel/saddle + caravan organization -> increased trade volume/range -> Mali facilitates and profits from wider exchange.**

### What is aligned

- First & 10 is strong on camel technology, caravan organization, gold/salt demand, Mali, Mansa Musa, and the consequences of trade.
- Skill Builder explicitly links technology, demand, political support, and effects.
- Checkpoints assess camel technology, goods/demand, Mali, and wider consequences.
- BeInTheRoom makes Mali's dependence on trade, route protection, and state policy concrete.
- Deep Reading/eBook is exceptionally coherent with the topic's causal structure.

### Minor issues

- Checkpoint language could more explicitly require **increased volume/geographical range**, not simply why trade existed.
- A renderer-config `contentDelivery` stable image points to Great Zimbabwe, which is a visual/content mismatch for Topic 2.4 even though the instructional text itself is strong.

### Required repair

Small wording and visual-asset cleanup only. Do not wholesale rewrite this topic.

## Topic 2.5 — Cultural Consequences of Connectivity

### Locked CED spine

**Connectivity diffuses cultural/technological traditions, changes urban fortunes, and produces more written travel accounts.**

Required anchors include Buddhism, Hinduism, Islam, paper, gunpowder, changing city fortunes/urbanization, and travelers Ibn Battuta, Margery Kempe, and Marco Polo.

### What is aligned

- Canonical targets and rebuilt Content Delivery now include all three historical developments.
- Religion/technology diffusion is deeply represented across all existing surfaces.
- Deep Reading contains strong material on how beliefs and technologies move and includes travelers such as Ibn Battuta and Marco Polo.

### Residual drift — largest in Unit 2

- **First & 10:** four sections are religion, technology, crop diffusion, and artistic/intellectual diffusion. It does not teach changing urban fortunes or travel accounts as required developments. It gives a full core section to crops, which belongs primarily in Topic 2.6.
- **Map:** religion/technology diffusion only; cities and travelers are absent.
- **Skill Builder:** focuses continuity/change in religion/technology. City growth/decline and travel writing are essentially absent.
- **Checkpoint 1:** religion only.
- **Checkpoint 2:** explicitly assesses “Technology and Agricultural Diffusion” and allows sugar/cotton/citrus — direct Topic 2.6 bleed.
- **Evidence Lab:** diffusion only; no city fortunes or travel-account evidence.
- **BeInTheRoom:** engaging scholar/idea-diffusion scenario, but it reinforces only KC-3.1.III.D.
- **Deep Reading/eBook:** sophisticated but still organized around religions, techniques, crops/material culture, and words/people. It contains travelers, but does not make the CED development “more travelers wrote accounts” explicit enough, omits Margery Kempe, and does not meaningfully teach KC-3.3.II on city decline/urbanization. Crop diffusion occupies major space.

### Required repair

This topic needs the biggest ecosystem rebuild. First & 10 and Deep Reading/eBook should become three clearly visible strands: **Ideas/technology move -> cities change -> travelers document the connected world**. Crop diffusion should move out of the core. Checkpoint 2 must stop assessing agricultural diffusion and instead assess city fortunes and/or travel accounts. Margery Kempe needs a visible place alongside Ibn Battuta and Marco Polo.

## Topic 2.6 — Environmental Consequences of Connectivity

### Locked CED spine

**Connectivity moves living things: crops and pathogens.**

Required crop anchors are bananas in Africa, new rice varieties in East Asia, and citrus in the Mediterranean, alongside epidemic disease/bubonic plague.

### What is aligned

- Canonical Content Delivery now puts crop and pathogen diffusion together.
- Checkpoint 2 explicitly requires one plague consequence and one crop example.
- First & 10 was partly repaired and now names all three required crops.
- Deep Reading/eBook contains a substantial “Species on the Move” section with bananas, rice, citrus, and pathogen movement.

### Residual drift

- **Map:** entirely plague geography.
- **BeSurreal:** plague-only.
- **Skill Builder:** plague causation/social catastrophe only.
- **Checkpoint 1:** plague-only, acceptable as one slice, but it leaves Checkpoint 2 carrying essentially all crop accountability.
- **Evidence Lab:** pandemic evidence only.
- **BeInTheRoom:** entirely the 1347 plague crisis. It is strong history, but contributes nothing to crop diffusion.
- **First & 10:** despite naming the crops, roughly three of four substantive sections are still plague; crop diffusion is compressed into the final section.
- **Deep Reading/eBook:** still frames the topic mainly as pandemic/ecological cost/evidence and points back to Topic 2.5 for crop chains, preserving the old content-location problem.

### Required repair

The ecosystem does not need to become 50/50 by word count, but students should encounter both branches repeatedly. Add crop diffusion to the Map, Skill Builder or Evidence Lab, and rebalance First & 10 so crops are a real explanatory strand rather than a final add-on. Deep Reading should move the named crop chains into Topic 2.6 instead of treating Topic 2.5 as their home.

## Topic 2.7 — Comparison of Economic Exchange

### Locked CED spine

Students compare the networks through the economic mechanisms emphasized across Unit 2: expanded interaction, commercial practices, transportation/commercial technology, luxury demand, productive capacity, and resulting diffusion.

### What is aligned

- Canonical Content Delivery is now built around a consistent comparison matrix.
- Skill Builder correctly insists on comparing the same category in two networks.
- Evidence Lab requires balanced evidence from different networks.
- Deep Reading/eBook is strong and unusually rigorous on transport constraints, commercial institutions, credit/partnerships, state roles, and interlocking networks.

### Residual drift

- **First & 10:** organizes comparison mainly around technology, goods, religions, cultural blending, and states/cities. Commercial practices and productive capacity are not prominent enough.
- **Skill Builder matrix:** useful, but it lacks explicit rows for commercial/financial practices and productive capacity/demand.
- **Checkpoint 1:** similarities emphasize luxury goods/religion/diaspora/connectivity rather than the CED economic mechanisms.
- **Checkpoint 2:** differences emphasize technology/geography/goods but do not require commercial practices or productive response.
- **BeInTheRoom:** renderer config has no URL, so the comparison scenario is not actually wired as a functioning module.

### Required repair

Add **Commercial practices/finance** and **Demand/productive capacity** to the comparison matrix and assessments. Update First & 10 around the same categories. Either build/wire the 2.7 BeInTheRoom comparison scenario or intentionally remove it from the required module path.

## Unit-level diagnosis

The second audit confirms the mechanism that allowed drift to survive the first repair:

**The canonical `lesson-2-x-*.js` files were corrected, but downstream `lesson-2-x-renderer-config.js`, standalone First & 10 readings, Deep Readings/eBook chapter content, checkpoints, and BeInTheRoom scenarios are independent instructional surfaces.**

Several of those surfaces still carry the previous topic architecture. That means a teacher can now open a CED-aligned Content Delivery deck while students complete modules that reinforce a different story.

The most important examples are:

- Topic 2.1 checkpoint work still teaches religion/technology/plague instead of commercial systems/cities/production.
- Topic 2.2 still under-teaches the three explicit CED cultural/technological transfer examples.
- Topic 2.5 still assigns major space to crop diffusion while omitting city fortunes and traveler accounts.
- Topic 2.6 still treats crop diffusion as secondary to a plague-centered ecosystem.
- Topic 2.7 still compares the networks more by visible characteristics than by the CED's specific economic mechanisms.

## Repair priority

1. **2.5** — major rebuild across First & 10, checkpoints, evidence, Deep Reading/eBook, and scenario framing.
2. **2.1** — align First & 10, Skill Builder, BeSurreal, and checkpoints with the new economic spine.
3. **2.2** — explicitly wire required transfer examples across reading, evidence, and assessment surfaces.
4. **2.6** — rebalance plague vs. crop diffusion.
5. **2.3** — restore omitted state/Zheng He/technology anchors in First & 10 and checkpoints.
6. **2.7** — add commercial-practice/productive-capacity comparison and wire BeInTheRoom.
7. **2.4** — minor wording/visual cleanup only.

## Safeguard required after repair

The current CED contract protects the **canonical topic model**. It should be followed by a second automated **instructional coherence contract** that verifies, by topic:

- First & 10 required concepts and banned cross-topic drift.
- Checkpoint-to-learning-target traceability.
- Skill Builder use of topic-valid evidence.
- Evidence Lab/Primary Source support of the topic spine.
- BeInTheRoom wiring and topic relevance.
- Deep Reading/eBook coverage of required CED developments without relocating another topic's core content.
- No required student-facing module can silently preserve an older topic architecture after the canonical lesson changes.

Until those secondary surfaces are repaired and gated, Unit 2 should be described as **100% CED-aligned at the canonical content layer, but not yet 100% instructionally coherent across the full student experience.**

## Sources leaned on, and in what role

- **Mun 1664:** Thomas Mun, *England's Treasure by Forraign Trade* (London, 1664), in W. J.
  Ashley's Economic Classics reprint (Macmillan, 1895), read from the archive.org text. Chapter 2,
  "The means to enrich the Kingdom, and to encrease our Treasure." The quotation is checked
  against that text letter for letter.
- **Colenbrander 1919:** H. T. Colenbrander, ed., *Jan Pieterszoon Coen: Bescheiden omtrent zijn
  bedrijf in Indië*, vol. 1 (The Hague, 1919), pp. 97 to 98, Coen's letter from Bantam of 27
  December 1614, read in the archive.org text. The Dutch: "Per experientie behoorden de heeren wel
  bekent te wesen, dat in Indien den handel gedreven ende gemainteneert moet worden onder
  beschuttinge ende faveur van U eygene wapenen, ende dat de wapenen gevoert moeten werden van de
  proffyten, die met den handel zijn genietende, in voegen dat den handel sonder d' oorloge, noch
  d' oorloge sonder den handel nyet gemainteneert connen werden." Every English quotation of Coen
  in the topic is BeHistorical's translation of this text. C. R. Boxer's often-quoted English
  (*The Dutch Seaborne Empire*, 1965) could not be opened this session, so it is not quoted.
- **Sahagún, Bustamante ed. 1830:** Bernardino de Sahagún, *Historia general de las cosas de
  Nueva España*, ed. Carlos María de Bustamante, vol. 3 (Mexico, 1830), pp. 321 to 322, appendix
  to Book 11 on idolatry, read in the archive.org text. The Tepeyac passage, including "tomada
  ocasión de los predicadores, que á Ntra Sra. la Madre de Dios la llamaban Tonantzin" and
  "Parece esta invención satánica para paliar la idolatría bajo la equivocación de éste nombre
  Tonantzin." Every English quotation of Sahagún in the topic is BeHistorical's translation.
- **Morga, Stanley tr. 1868:** Antonio de Morga, *The Philippine Islands, Moluccas, Siam, Cambodia,
  Japan, and China, at the Close of the Sixteenth Century*, translated by Henry E. J. Stanley
  (Hakluyt Society, London, 1868), pp. 336 to 340, chapter 8, from *Sucesos de las Islas
  Filipinas* (Mexico, 1609). Read in the Google Books text on archive.org. Public domain.
- **Flynn and Giráldez:** Arturo Giráldez, *Born with a Silver Spoon: China, American Silver and
  Global Markets during the Early Modern Period* (doctoral thesis, Universiteit van Amsterdam,
  1999), which reprints Flynn and Giráldez, "Born with a 'Silver Spoon': The Origin of World
  Trade in 1571," *Journal of World History* 6 (1995), and related articles. Read in the UvA-DARE
  PDF. The specialist source for silver's flow to China; its figures cite Barrett (1990), Cross
  (1983) and Von Glahn (1996).
- **Schurz 1939, as cited:** William Lytle Schurz, *The Manila Galleon* (1939), for the 1593
  decree, as cited in the ANU Press chapter "Manila and Macao"
  (press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p66561/mobile/ch06s04.html) and in an MPRA working
  paper, "Shipwrecked by Rents" (mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/102974). Secondary citations of a
  standard work.
- **Eltis, Slave Voyages:** David Eltis, "A Brief Overview of the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade,"
  slavevoyages.org essays; and the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database for the sex ratio, with
  the published summaries of Eltis and Engerman's work on sex and age ratios (Economic History
  Review, 1993) as reported in secondary summaries.
- **Manning 1990 and Fenske 2013, as summarized:** Patrick Manning, *Slavery and African Life*
  (1990), the demographic model of the slave trade's effect on African sex ratios and polygyny;
  James Fenske, "Does Land Abundance Explain African Institutions?" and his micro-level work,
  summarized as finding the long-run polygyny evidence "tenuous"; and Edoardo Teso, "The
  Long-Term Effect of Demographic Shocks on the Evolution of Gender Roles: Evidence from the
  Transatlantic Slave Trade" (Journal of the European Economic Association, 2019), on women's
  agricultural work. Read through summaries, so the family changes are stated as historians'
  arguments.
- **Games 2020:** Alison Games, *Inventing the English Massacre: Amboyna in History and Memory*
  (Oxford, 2020), through its publisher's abstract and reviews; with the Itinerario article on
  the Japanese mercenaries (Cambridge Core). For the 1623 numbers and the doubt about the plot.
- **Encyclopedia and summary sources, for routine chronology only:** Encyclopaedia Britannica and
  Wikipedia summaries of the Battle of Diu (1509), the Siege of Diu (1538), the Moroccan invasion
  of Songhai and Tondibi (anchored in Lansiné Kaba, *Journal of African History*, 1981), the
  Calico Acts, Francisco de Ávila and the extirpation campaigns (anchored in Duviols 1971), the
  VOC's charter (with Rupert Gerritsen's English translation of the 1602 charter), and the
  English East India Company's charter.

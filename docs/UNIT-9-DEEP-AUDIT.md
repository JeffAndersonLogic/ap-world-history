## Executive finding

Unit 9 divides into two quality bands. Topics 9.1–9.3 and 9.9 are strong, content-specific builds. Topics 9.4–9.8 use a newer generic “evidence + qualify” template. That template produces polished AP-style reasoning practice, but in 9.4–9.7 it sometimes compresses multi-branch College Board content into two favored examples and turns a content target into a generic complexity target.

## Topics 9.1–9.3

These are strong content-specific builds.

### 9.1
All five required technology branches are represented:
- communication/transport;
- energy;
- birth control;
- Green Revolution/commercial agriculture;
- medical innovation.

The three-target grouping is sensible and preserves the official topic.

### 9.2
The lesson accurately organizes the official disease development into:
- persistent diseases associated with poverty;
- emergent epidemics;
- diseases with higher incidence because of increased longevity.

Preserve.

### 9.3
The lesson directly protects:
- deforestation/desertification/air quality/freshwater use;
- increased resource competition;
- greenhouse gases/pollution and climate-change debate.

Preserve while keeping climate claims source-based and historically framed.

## Topic 9.4 — Economics in the Global Age

### What is good

The runtime CED metadata contains all three required developments:
- free-market liberalization;
- knowledge economies + manufacturing shifts toward Asia/Latin America;
- economic institutions, multinational corporations, and regional trade agreements.

### What needs repair

The formal instructional/assessment structure is narrower than that metadata.

- Target 2 explicitly names only Deng-era reforms and Bangladesh garment manufacturing.
- Target 3 is a generic “qualify an argument” target rather than protecting another content branch.
- Checkpoint 1 requires Deng + Bangladesh; NAFTA and multinational corporations appear only as terms.
- The objective is **continuity and change**, but the lesson needs a clearer continuity baseline rather than treating qualification as a substitute for continuity.

### Repair target

Protect three content branches before the reasoning move:
**liberalization -> production/knowledge geography -> institutions/MNCs/trade agreements**, then require an explicit continuity/change claim.

## Topic 9.5 — Calls for Reform and Responses After 1900

### What is good

The metadata contains all three required developments:
- rights-based challenges;
- broader access to education/political/professional roles;
- movements protesting unequal environmental/economic effects of integration.

### What needs repair

- Target 2 foregrounds the UDHR, feminism, and education.
- Target 3 becomes generic qualification.
- Checkpoint 1 explicitly asks only about the UDHR and feminism/education, while Green Belt/fair-trade evidence appears only in the terms list.

The environmental/economic protest branch therefore lacks equal formal assessment weight.

### Repair target

Use three explicit content moves:
**rights discourse -> inclusion/access -> environmental/economic justice movements**, then add complexity.

## Topic 9.6 — Globalized Culture After 1900

### What is good

All official KCs are present in metadata:
- popular/consumer culture becoming more global;
- arts/entertainment reflecting global society;
- consumer culture crossing borders.

### What needs repair

The checkpoint formally requires Reggae and Bollywood. World Cup/Olympic spectacle and global consumer examples such as Alibaba/Toyota/Coca-Cola are only optional terms.

That means the **consumer-culture branch is present but under-assessed**.

### Repair target

Require evidence from at least two distinct cultural mechanisms:
1. media/arts/sports circulation;
2. consumer brands/online commerce.

Students should explain **how and why** globalization changed culture, not merely prove that cultural products traveled.

## Topic 9.7 — Resistance to Globalization After 1900

The official topic is compact: responses to rising cultural and economic globalization took varied forms.

The current lesson is heavily economic:
- anti-IMF;
- anti-World Bank;
- Seattle WTO.

Locally developed social media such as Weibo is present in metadata/map material but not central to the checkpoint.

### Light repair target

Require students to distinguish at least two types of response, ideally one economic/institutional response and one cultural/digital/localization response, so “various responses” is actually demonstrated.

## Topic 9.8 — Institutions Developing in a Globalized World

The governing content is intentionally narrow: new international organizations, including the UN, formed to promote peace/cooperation.

The current General Assembly/Security Council/veto evidence is well chosen. Peacekeeping and humanitarian/development agencies are useful enrichment as long as the core claim remains why globalization changed state interaction through international organizations. Preserve.

## Topic 9.9 — Continuity and Change in a Globalized World

The current capstone directly matches Learning Objective I:
**extent to which science and technology brought change from 1900 to the present.**

It draws from Unit 9 technology, agriculture, medicine, economics, rights, and culture while requiring qualification/continuity. Preserve.

No BeInTheRoom is required merely because the topic is a synthesis capstone.

## Repair order

1. **9.4** — restore all global-economy branches and explicit continuity/change.
2. **9.5** — formally assess environmental/economic justice responses.
3. **9.6** — formally assess consumer culture as well as arts/media.
4. **9.7** — require more than one type of globalization response.
5. Preserve 9.1–9.3, 9.8, 9.9.

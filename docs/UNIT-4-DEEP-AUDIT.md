## Protected Unit 4 instructional spine

The repaired unit should preserve these College Board topic jobs:

- **4.1:** cross-cultural diffusion of knowledge/technology -> ships, navigation tools, and wind/current knowledge -> transoceanic travel and trade.
- **4.2:** state-supported exploration + economic causes/effects -> Portuguese, Spanish, English, French, and Dutch maritime exploration.
- **4.3:** Columbian Exchange -> plants, animals, disease vectors/diseases, and demographic/environmental consequences in both hemispheres.
- **4.4:** maritime empire formation/expansion + continuity/change in economic and labor systems + continuity/change in slavery.
- **4.5:** economic strategies of rulers + continuity/change in exchange networks + political/economic/cultural effects on society + similarities/differences in the effects of belief systems.
- **4.6:** effects of state expansion/centralization -> local resistance and organized resistance by enslaved people.
- **4.7:** maintenance/change in social categories, roles, and practices -> accommodation/suppression of diversity, new elites/casta, changing power of existing elites.
- **4.8:** use Unit 4 evidence to explain **how economic developments from 1450–1750 affected social structures over time**.

## Topic 4.1 — Technological Innovations

### What is aligned

- Canonical targets contain KC-4.1.II and KC-4.1.II.A.
- Checkpoint 1 correctly asks students to identify maritime technologies and explain the navigation problem each solved.

### What needs repair

1. **Checkpoint 2 is not aligned to the targets it claims to assess.**  
   It claims Learning Targets 2–3 but asks about portolan/Waldseemüller maps, printing, and state sponsorship. The official topic requires cross-cultural diffusion of Classical/Islamic/Asian knowledge plus tools, ship design, and wind/current knowledge.
2. **State sponsorship belongs primarily to 4.2.**
3. The lecture gives the printing press and cartography more instructional weight than the required cross-cultural diffusion mechanism.

### Repair target

Make the second half answer:
**Where did navigational knowledge come from, and how did adopted knowledge + ship design + wind/current knowledge make ocean travel possible?**

## Topic 4.2 — Exploration: Causes and Events

### What is aligned

- State sponsorship and the Portuguese/Spanish exploration story are present.
- Checkpoint 1 establishes motives and links them to state action.

### What needs repair

1. **Checkpoint 2 does not assess the full northern Atlantic branch.**  
   The target says English, French, and Dutch sponsorship of northern crossings, but the checkpoint substitutes Treaty of Tordesillas analysis and non-European perspective.
2. **Economic causes and effects need to remain the formal reasoning center.**  
   Religious/political motives are useful enrichment, but should not obscure the College Board economic cause/effect task.
3. The “Ottoman conquest of Constantinople disrupted/closed overland trade and therefore caused exploration” story is too strong and should be removed or carefully qualified.

### Repair target

Students should be able to connect **state sponsorship -> specific voyages/routes -> trade/travel effects**, including Portuguese, Spanish, English, French, and Dutch cases.

## Topic 4.3 — Columbian Exchange

### What is aligned

- Crop/animal exchange and epidemic disease are prominent.
- Checkpoint 1 is strongly aligned to biological transfers.
- Checkpoint 2 correctly asks students to explain disease vulnerability and demographic collapse.

### What needs repair

1. **Checkpoint 2 travels too far into later Unit 4 topics.**  
   It requires a direct chain from Indigenous demographic collapse to the Atlantic slave trade and then adds Potosí silver/global trade. Those belong primarily to labor/slavery and global-circulation content in 4.4–4.5.
2. The official Columbian Exchange topic requires full two-hemisphere exchange effects, including Old World crops/animals and foods carried by enslaved Africans; these should not be crowded out by silver.
3. Any disease -> labor -> slavery causal chain should be carefully qualified rather than presented as a single sufficient cause.

### Repair target

Keep the checkpoint on **transfer -> mechanism -> demographic/environmental consequence**, using plants, animals, disease, and diet/population effects.

## Topic 4.4 — Maritime Empires Established

### What is aligned

- The runtime CED metadata includes the major required developments.
- Portuguese trading-post and Spanish territorial models are strong examples.
- The BeInTheRoom scenario reinforces Indian Ocean strategy.

### What needs repair

The College Board topic is broader than “three European models.”

1. **African state growth is underrepresented in targets/assessment.**  
   Kongo and Asante participation in expanding trading networks is part of the required state-building story.
2. **Indian Ocean continuity is not protected as a learning target/checkpoint.**  
   Existing intra-Asian trade and Asian merchants continued despite European disruption.
3. **Labor-system continuity/change is not fully assessed.**  
   Existing systems such as the Incan mit’a and newer systems such as chattel slavery, indentured servitude, encomienda, and hacienda belong in this topic.
4. **Slavery continuity/change is not protected.**  
   Traditional African forms continued while plantation demand expanded enslaved labor in the Americas.
5. The current checkpoints focus Portuguese vs. Spanish vs. joint-stock empire models; joint-stock/mercantilist strategy belongs more centrally to 4.5.

### Repair target

Organize 4.4 around:
**new maritime empires/states -> what changed in trade/labor/slavery -> what continued despite European expansion**.

## Topic 4.5 — Maritime Empires Maintained and Developed

### What is aligned

- Runtime CED metadata contains the required H/I/J/K development clusters.
- Mercantilism, joint-stock companies, silver, Atlantic exchange, and cultural mixing are present somewhere in the lesson ecosystem.

### What needs repair

This is one of the clearest mismatches in Unit 4.

1. **The canonical learning targets cover only part of the official topic.**  
   They include mercantilism, silver/global circulation, and Atlantic labor/cultural synthesis, but do not protect:
   - peasant/artisan labor intensification,
   - gender/family restructuring in Africa,
   - the belief-system comparison/syncretism branch.
2. **Checkpoint 1 is directly miswired.**  
   It claims Learning Targets 1–2 but asks about the mita at Potosí, viceregal administration, the Catholic Church, and encomienda/mita comparison. That does not assess mercantilism/joint-stock strategy or global silver circulation.
3. **Checkpoint 2 is also miswired.**  
   It claims Learning Target 3 (Atlantic labor/cultural synthesis) but asks almost entirely about silver routes and Chinese demand.
4. The lesson subtitle and lecture spine center forced labor, colonial administration, and silver rather than the actual four-objective 4.5 structure.

### Repair target

Use four explicit moves:
**economic strategy -> exchange network change/continuity -> social effects -> belief-system effects**.

## Topic 4.6 — Internal and External Challenges to State Power

### What is aligned

- Pueblo resistance and maroon resistance are excellent required examples.
- The first two canonical targets map correctly to KC-4.3.III.iii and KC-5.3.III.C.
- Checkpoint 1 is well aligned to the actual topic.

### What needs repair

1. **Learning Target 3 is an adjacent-topic addition, not a 4.6 requirement.**  
   It uses KC-4.3.III.ii (economic disputes/rivalries), while the current CED gives Topic 4.6 KC-4.3.III.iii and KC-5.3.III.C.
2. **Checkpoint 2 therefore assesses the wrong topic job.**  
   Dutch-Portuguese competition, privateering, and the Seven Years' War are external European rivalries better housed in 4.5/economic rivalry, not the required 4.6 resistance objective.
3. The structural scanner correctly surfaced the stray KC mapping; human review confirms it is real drift rather than a harmless metadata issue.

### Repair target

Remove the third target/checkpoint branch and make 4.6 entirely about:
**state expansion/centralization -> local resistance + organized resistance by enslaved people**.

## Topic 4.7 — Changing Social Hierarchies

### What is aligned

- The canonical three developments match the current CED:
  - accommodation/suppression of diversity,
  - formation of new elites/casta,
  - changing power of existing elites.
- Checkpoint 2 is a useful continuity/change exercise.

### What needs repair

1. **Checkpoint 1 claims to assess Targets 1–2 but only asks about casta.**  
   It does not require accommodation/suppression of ethnic or religious groups.
2. The lesson over-centers racial classification in Spanish America. Casta should remain important, but not replace Ottoman/Mughal/Qing diversity policy or the changing position of existing elites.
3. Gender can remain enrichment where it is grounded in the CED examples, but the assessment must first cover the three required developments.

### Repair target

Use a three-part hierarchy story:
**states classify/treat groups differently -> new elites form -> existing elites gain/lose influence**.

## Topic 4.8 — Continuity and Change from 1450 to 1750

### What the current CED requires

The current Learning Objective N is specific: students should explain **how economic developments from 1450 to 1750 affected social structures over time**. The topic is also an argumentation/synthesis opportunity using Unit 4 evidence.

### What needs repair

The current BeHistorical topic is built around a different synthesis question:
- global trade changes,
- Columbian Exchange demographic/environmental changes,
- continuities in Indian Ocean/Asian/Islamic commerce.

Those are useful Unit 4 review materials, but they do not answer the current governing learning objective.

The current targets, success criteria, lecture, and checkpoints therefore need a **topic-spine rebuild**, not a small wording patch.

### Repair target

Rebuild 4.8 around a defensible CCOT argument such as:

**Economic change (global trade, plantation economies, coerced labor, silver/mercantilism) -> changes and continuities in social hierarchy, labor status, gender/family patterns, elites, and racial categories.**

Students should use evidence from multiple earlier Unit 4 topics to argue what changed, what persisted, and why.

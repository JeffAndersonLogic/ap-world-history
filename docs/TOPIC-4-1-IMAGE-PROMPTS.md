Rules these pictures live under (`docs/PRESENTATION-AUTHORING.md`, "AI-generated images"):

- On a slide each one carries exactly `Historical Reconstruction - AI Generated`, small, in the
  credit. Prefer a clean file with no text printed into the picture.
- **Never in the Evidence Lab**, and never called evidence anywhere, slide notes included. Real

- A picture that fails a "check before use" item is regenerated or dropped, not captioned around.

No generator can promise accuracy. The prompts below name the period details and list the
common errors to exclude; the check after generation is what actually makes a picture usable.

## Shared style line (already included in each prompt)

## 1. The Problem: a caravel fighting the wind home (beats 4 and 5)

**What it teaches:** going south was easy; coming home meant fighting the trade wind.

> Photorealistic cinematic historical reconstruction, 16:9 landscape. A small Portuguese caravel of the 1440s, about 20 meters long, beating north along the desert coast of northwest Africa against a strong, steady northeast wind. The caravel has two masts carrying only triangular lateen sails of plain, weathered off-white canvas, a low hull of dark tarred wood, a raised stern deck, and is steered by a long wooden tiller at the stern. The ship heels hard over, spray breaking across the bow, sails straining. Eight to ten barefoot sailors in coarse undyed wool tunics, loose breeches and knitted caps haul on lines. To the right, a low, empty coast of sand dunes and pale rock cliffs under a hazy sky tinted with Saharan dust. Late afternoon light, choppy blue-gray Atlantic with whitecaps all running toward the ship. Mood: hard, slow work against the wind. Documentary realism, no text, no watermark.
>
> Exclude: square sails, a ship's steering wheel, rows of cannon or gun ports, crosses or flags on the sails, tricorn hats, pirate costumes, palm trees, a modern lighthouse, any land vegetation.

**Check before use:** lateen sails only (early exploration caravels were lateen-rigged); tiller,
not a wheel (the ship's wheel is an 18th-century invention); no guns; desert coast, not jungle.

## 2. Borrowed Knowledge: the translators of Toledo (beat 6)

**What it teaches:** knowledge moved with people; a Greek book, kept in Arabic, reaches Latin.

> Photorealistic cinematic historical reconstruction, 16:9 landscape, set in Toledo, Spain, around 1250. A quiet stone study with horseshoe arches and walls decorated with carved geometric plaster and patterned glazed tiles in the Mudéjar style. Morning light falls through a small arched window onto a heavy wooden table. On the left, an older Jewish scholar in a plain dark wool robe and soft cap reads aloud from a large handwritten Arabic manuscript of astronomy, its pages showing columns of writing and a circular star diagram, the text soft and out of focus. On the right, a Christian cleric in a simple brown habit writes in Latin with a quill on parchment. Between them on the table sits a polished brass astrolabe of Islamic workmanship, a flat disc with a pierced, lacy star map on its face, beside an inkwell and a candle. Calm, focused, two people working together. Documentary realism, no readable text, no watermark.
>
> Exclude: printed books, eyeglasses, paper notebooks with lines, Gothic stained glass, maps of the Americas, telescopes, globes, anyone in a turban drawn as a costume, any readable lettering.

**Check before use:** manuscripts, not printed books (printing in Europe is c. 1450); the
astrolabe is the flat planispheric kind, which is correct here on land; script blurred so no
invented letters can be read; no badges or caricatured clothing. The reading says Christian
and Jewish translators worked from Arabic books, so the picture shows exactly that.

## 3. A New Tool: the mariner's astrolabe at noon (beat 6)

**What it teaches:** Europeans adapted a borrowed instrument into a new tool to find latitude.

> Photorealistic cinematic historical reconstruction, 16:9 landscape, the deck of a Portuguese caravel off the coast of West Africa in the 1480s, at noon under a high bright sun. In the foreground a weathered Portuguese pilot in a dark wool doublet, linen shirt and soft cap holds up a heavy cast-brass mariner's astrolabe, a thick open ring about 20 centimeters across with cut-out spokes and a weighted bottom, hanging from his thumb by a small ring at the top so it stays upright. He tilts the pointer arm so a pinpoint of sunlight passes through one small sight hole and lands on the other; he is not looking at the sun. Beside him a younger sailor holds open a small handwritten booklet of number tables and a wax tablet. Behind them, lateen sails and rigging, and a distant green coastline. Strong overhead shadows, glittering sea. Documentary realism, no readable text, no watermark.
>
> Exclude: a telescope, a sextant or octant, a compass rose painted on the deck, a ship's steering wheel, a flat lacy decorative astrolabe, printed books, tricorn hats, anyone staring directly into the sun.

**Check before use:** the instrument is an open, heavy ring with spokes (the mariner's type),
not the flat decorative kind from image 2; the sun is sighted through the holes, never looked at;
tables are handwritten (Zacuto's printed almanac comes in 1496); no telescope or sextant (both
later inventions).

## 4. The Wind Wheel: sailing away to get home (beat 8)

**What it teaches:** the *volta do mar*; to get home, a ship sails out into open ocean.

> Photorealistic cinematic historical reconstruction, 16:9 landscape, high wide aerial view. A single small Portuguese caravel of the late 1400s with triangular lateen sails sails alone across an immense, empty open Atlantic Ocean, heading away from Africa toward the northwest, no land anywhere on the horizon. Long rows of puffy white trade-wind clouds stretch across a pale blue sky, and long ocean swells roll under a steady wind, the ship leaning gently and leaving a thin white wake. Deep blue water, bright midday light, a sense of enormous distance and isolation. The ship is small in the frame to show the scale of the ocean. Documentary realism, no text, no watermark.
>
> Exclude: any land, islands or coastline, other ships, storms, whales or sea monsters, square-rigged galleons, cannon, flags with modern designs.

**Check before use:** no land at all (the point is that the ship sailed away from it); one small
lateen-rigged caravel; the slide adds the route arrows, not the picture.

## 5. The Payoff and the Twist: arriving at Calicut, 1498 (beats 9 and 10)

**What it teaches:** a new sea route reached an ocean world that was already busy, and the
Portuguese arrived as newcomers to it.

> Photorealistic cinematic historical reconstruction, 16:9 landscape, the open roadstead off Calicut on the Malabar coast of India in late May 1498. In the middle distance, three weather-beaten Portuguese ships ride at anchor: two large carracks with high, rounded fore and stern castles, square sails furled on two masts and a lateen sail on the rear mast, and one smaller lateen-rigged caravel, their hulls salt-stained after ten months at sea. All around them, and much more numerous, are Indian Ocean trading ships: dhows with sewn wooden planks and big triangular lateen sails, and small local boats rowing cargo and people between the ships and the shore. Behind them, a long sandy beach lined with coconut palms, wooden warehouses and houses with steep, tiered, sloping tiled and thatched roofs, and a mosque in the local Kerala style with a sloping tiled roof and no dome. Heavy gray pre-monsoon clouds build over green hills. Documentary realism, no text, no watermark.
>
> Exclude: Chinese junks, a stone harbor or long pier with ships docked against it, domed mosques or minarets, Mughal-style architecture, European-style buildings on shore, cannon firing, flags with crosses, modern ships.

**Check before use:** the Portuguese ships anchor offshore (Calicut had no deep harbor, so cargo
moved by small boat); local ships outnumber the Portuguese; **no Chinese junks** (the Ming
voyages had ended in 1433); Kerala-style roofs, not domes; three Portuguese ships (two naus and
a caravel, as da Gama's fleet was by then).

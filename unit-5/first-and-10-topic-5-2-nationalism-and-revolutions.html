First & 10 Reading

How Revolutions Made Nations

By 1789 the new ideas about rights were everywhere educated people read. But ideas alone never overthrew a king. This reading follows four revolutions, in America, France, Haiti, and Spanish America, and asks what caused each one and what it created. Then it follows the biggest thing they left behind: the nation, a new loyalty that joined some countries together and broke others apart.

1

The Question

What Does It Take to Start a Revolution?

Topic 5.1 ended with a question. By 1789 the new ideas about rights were everywhere educated people read. In America, France, Haiti, and Spanish America, people turned them into revolutions. What caused those revolutions, and what did they create?

Start with this: ideas are not enough. Many people read John Locke and went on obeying their kings. A revolution needed three things at the same time. First, a grievance: a wrong that many people felt, such as an unfair tax or a law that shut them out. Second, the words to name it, written down in a document people could read aloud and rally around. Third, a chance: a moment when the old ruler was broke, far away, or gone.

Look for all three in each revolution below. And watch for one more thing: each revolution helped cause the next one.

AP Thinking, Causation

Ideas are one cause, not the cause. The College Board says Enlightenment ideas "influenced resistance" to kings and empires. People rebelled when they also had a grievance and a chance. A strong causation answer names more than one cause.

2

America

A Republic With No King, 1775 to 1783

The grievance. Britain won the Seven Years' War in 1763, but it came out of the war deep in debt. To help pay, Parliament taxed its thirteen American colonies, with laws such as the Stamp Act of 1765. The colonists had no members in Parliament, so they protested: "no taxation without representation."

The words. On 4 July 1776 the colonies' Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence. It said "that all men are created equal," that governments get "their just powers from the consent of the governed," and that when a government destroys people's rights, "it is the Right of the People to alter or to abolish it."

The chance. Britain was an ocean away, and in 1778 France joined the war on the American side.

What it created. By 1783 the colonies had won their independence as the United States. In 1787 they wrote a constitution for a republic: a country with no king, whose leaders are elected. The new republic became a model that later revolutionaries pointed to. But it kept slavery, and most states let only men who owned property vote.

AP Thinking, Causation

Sort your causes. A long-term cause builds up over years, like Britain's war debt and the taxes that followed. A short-term cause sets things off, like the moment France joined the war. Naming both makes your explanation stronger.

3

France

The Bill Comes Due, 1789 to 1799

Here is the first link in the chain. The chance. Helping the Americans win had cost France a fortune, and by 1788 King Louis XVI's government was nearly bankrupt. To raise taxes, he called the Estates-General, an assembly of the clergy, the nobles, and everyone else. It had not met since 1614.

The grievance. The clergy and the nobles paid little in taxes. Everyone else, the Third Estate, paid most of them, and after a bad harvest in 1788 the price of bread soared. In June 1789 the Third Estate declared itself the National Assembly, and on 14 July a Paris crowd stormed the Bastille, a royal fortress and prison.

The words. In August 1789 the Assembly ended the nobles' special privileges and adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen. "Men are born and remain free and equal in rights," it began, and "the principle of all sovereignty resides essentially in the nation." Sovereignty means the highest power in a state.

What it created. France was not trying to break away from anyone; it was remaking a kingdom. In 1792 it abolished the monarchy and became a republic, and in 1793 it executed the king. During the Terror (1793 to 1794) the government killed thousands of people it called enemies of the revolution. In 1799 a general, Napoleon Bonaparte, took power, and in 1804 he crowned himself emperor. His armies carried the revolution's laws across Europe, and they also made many Europeans hate French rule.

AP Thinking, Causation

An effect can become a cause. France's help won the American war. That help left France nearly bankrupt, and the bankruptcy forced the king to call the assembly where the French Revolution began. One revolution's effect became the next one's cause.

4

Haiti

The Words, Claimed by the Enslaved, 1791 to 1804

The grievance. Saint-Domingue, on the Caribbean island of Hispaniola, was France's most valuable colony. Its sugar and coffee were grown by enslaved Africans. Around 1789 it held more than 400,000 enslaved people and fewer than 80,000 free people: more than five enslaved people for every free person.

The words. Saint-Domingue's free people of color, free people of African or mixed descent, asked the Assembly in Paris for the equal rights its declaration promised. They were refused.

The chance. With France in revolution and the colony's white planters divided, enslaved people in the northern plain rose up in August 1791. Toussaint Louverture, who had been born enslaved and had won his freedom before the revolution, became its most famous leader. In 1793 he announced: "I want Liberty and Equality to reign in Saint Domingue." Fighting to keep the colony, French officials there began freeing enslaved people in 1793, and in February 1794 the government in Paris abolished slavery in all French colonies.

Then Napoleon tried to turn back the clock. In 1802 he brought slavery back in other French colonies, such as Guadeloupe, and sent an army to retake Saint-Domingue. Toussaint was captured and died in a French prison in 1803. His officer Jean-Jacques Dessalines fought on, and yellow fever and the war destroyed the French army.

What it created. On 1 January 1804 Dessalines declared independence and gave the country its old Taíno name, Haiti. It was the second independent country in the Americas, and the first founded by people who had freed themselves from slavery. Its effects reached far. Having lost Saint-Domingue, Napoleon sold Louisiana to the United States in 1803. Countries that allowed slavery feared Haiti; the United States did not recognize it until 1862, and in 1825 France demanded 150 million francs, later cut to 90 million, as the price of recognizing Haiti's independence.

AP Thinking, Causation

Who caused it? France's 1794 law did not hand Haiti its freedom. Enslaved people rose up first, in 1791, and the law followed the fighting. Then Haitians won their independence by defeating Napoleon's army. When you explain a cause, say who acted.

5

Spanish America

An Empire With No King, 1810 to 1826

The grievance. In Spain's American empire, creoles, people of Spanish descent born in the Americas, owned land and businesses but were kept out of the highest offices. Those went to peninsulares, men born in Spain. Spain also controlled what the colonies could grow, make, and trade. Simón Bolívar, a creole from Caracas, wrote that Spain "has kept us in a sort of permanent infancy with regard to public affairs": treated like children, never allowed to govern.

The chance. Here is another link in the chain. In 1808 Napoleon invaded Spain and forced its king from the throne. Spanish America suddenly had no king to obey. In 1810 creole leaders formed governments of their own in Caracas and Buenos Aires, and in Mexico a priest, Miguel Hidalgo, called on Native and mestizo villagers to rise up.

The words. In 1815, beaten and in exile on the British island of Jamaica, Bolívar wrote the Letter from Jamaica. "The hatred that the Peninsula has inspired in us," he wrote, "is greater than the ocean between us." The tie that bound Spanish America to Spain, he argued, "has been severed." Then Haiti, the last revolution's creation, helped the next one. In 1816 Haiti's president, Alexandre Pétion, gave Bolívar guns, supplies, and soldiers for a new expedition, and asked him in return to end slavery in the lands he freed.

What it created. By 1826 Spain had lost every colony in the Americas except Cuba and Puerto Rico. Mexico, Gran Colombia, Peru, Chile, and others became independent countries, and Brazil broke away from Portugal in 1822 with an emperor of its own. Creole elites took the top places. Most of the new countries kept a sharp social hierarchy, and slavery lasted in several of them for decades. Bolívar himself doubted that a government chosen entirely by the people, "wholly representative," suited Spanish America yet.

AP Thinking, Claims and Evidence in Sources

Compare two arguments. The Declaration of Independence and the Letter from Jamaica both argue that a far-off ruler has lost the right to rule. But the 1776 declaration rests on the people's consent, and Bolívar doubted his people were ready to govern themselves fully. To compare sources, say how their arguments are alike, how they differ, and why.

6

New Ideas

New Ways to Govern, and a New Loyalty

Behind all four revolutions was anger at kings and empires. The revolutions left behind new answers to an old question: how should a country be run? One answer was the republic, with a written constitution. Another was liberalism: the belief that a constitution and an elected parliament should limit the government and protect each person's rights, such as free speech, a free press, and property. Many liberals in the 1800s still wanted only men with property to vote. A third was democracy, rule by the people, which pushed for the vote to reach all men. These ideas set off new revolutions across Europe in 1830 and 1848. Most of the uprisings of 1848 were crushed within two years.

The revolutions also said that a state belongs to its people. That raised a new question: which people? More and more, people answered with the nation, everyone who shared a language, a religion, customs, and a homeland. That feeling, nationalism, put loyalty to the nation above loyalty to a king or an empire.

Governments learned to use it. In 1793, at war with much of Europe, revolutionary France ordered that "all Frenchmen are in permanent requisition for the service of the armies." Young men would fight, married men would make weapons, women would make tents and serve in hospitals, and old people would go to public squares to preach "the unity of the Republic." The whole nation was called to war, not just a king's soldiers. But nationalism could also be turned against governments, by people who felt they were a nation without a country of their own.

AP Thinking, Causation

Effects keep going. The revolutions caused new ideas, liberalism, democracy, and nationalism, and those ideas then caused new revolutions. When you explain effects, follow them forward: what did this effect go on to cause?

7

Nations

Nationalism Redraws the Map

Through the 1800s, people who saw themselves as one nation wanted one state, with borders to match. Sometimes that meant unification: joining many small states into one. Italy was split into several states. Italian nationalists, among them the statesman Count Cavour and the soldier Giuseppe Garibaldi, joined most of it into one kingdom in 1861, and added Rome in 1870. Germany was split into more than thirty states. In 1848, German liberals met in Frankfurt to unite Germany with an elected parliament and a constitution, and failed. Then Otto von Bismarck, the chief minister of Prussia, told Prussia's lawmakers in 1862 that the great questions of the time are not decided "by speeches and majority resolutions," which had been "the big mistake of 1848 and 1849," but "by iron and blood." After three wars, the German Empire was proclaimed on 18 January 1871, in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, the palace Louis XIV built.

Sometimes nationalism broke a state apart. The Ottoman Empire ruled many peoples in the Balkans, in southeastern Europe. Serbs rebelled and won the right to rule themselves. Greeks rose up in 1821, and in 1830 Britain, France, and Russia declared Greece an independent kingdom. Other Balkan peoples followed, and in 1878 Serbia, Montenegro, and Romania were recognized as independent.

The Ottoman government answered with a loyalty of its own, Ottomanism. In 1856 it promised equal treatment to all its subjects, Muslim or not, and in 1869 a law made every subject an Ottoman citizen. The idea was one loyalty for everyone, to the empire, instead of to a nation. It did not stop the Balkan nations from leaving.

The idea reached beyond Europe, too. In the Spanish-ruled Philippines in the 1880s and 1890s, Filipino writers in the Propaganda Movement, among them José Rizal, demanded equal rights with Spaniards. Spain executed Rizal in 1896, during a revolution against its rule.

So, what caused the revolutions, and what did they create? A grievance, the words to name it, and a chance to act, in America, France, Haiti, and Spanish America, and each revolution's effects helped cause the next. They created new independent countries in the Americas, republics and constitutions, and new ideas about government, liberalism and democracy. And they created the nation as the thing a state was supposed to belong to, an idea that kept joining some states together and pulling others apart long after the last Spanish colony on the mainland broke free.

While Paris argued about rights, a different revolution was starting in British workshops. That is Topic 5.3.

AP Thinking, Causation

One cause, two effects. Nationalism joined Italy and Germany together and broke Greece and Serbia away from the Ottoman Empire. A cause can push in opposite directions. Saying both makes a stronger explanation.

BeReady: 10-Second Takeaway

Ideas alone did not make a revolution. In America, France, Haiti, and Spanish America, revolution came when people had a grievance, the words to name it in a document such as the Declaration of Independence, the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, or Bolívar's Letter from Jamaica, and a chance to act. Each revolution helped cause the next: the American war bankrupted France, France's declaration was claimed in Saint-Domingue, Napoleon's invasion of Spain opened the way in Spanish America, and Haiti armed Bolívar. The revolutions created new independent countries in the Americas, republics and constitutions, and new ideas, liberalism and democracy. They also created nationalism, a loyalty to the nation that governments used and that joined Italy and Germany together while it broke the Balkans away from the Ottoman Empire. Grievance + words + a chance = revolution. Then the nation redrew the map.

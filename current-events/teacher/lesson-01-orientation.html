Lesson 1 Teacher Orientation

Reverse History: The Ban in Your Locker. Everything on this page is for you, not for the room. Read the flags before you run the lesson the first time.

The trace, in one line

A locked pouch in Zionsville, to a teacher who could not enforce the old rule, to a national movement, to a book that told a story about teen mental health, to a saturation point around 2012, to a product launch in 2007.

The one refinement to Step 6

The 2007 iPhone is the origin only as a necessary condition. It becomes the origin of this event once you add two later pieces, the App Store (2008) and the front-facing camera (2010). Keeping that precision is what stops Step 6 from collapsing into "phones bad, phones started in 2007". The student-facing text in Step 6 already makes this move explicitly.

Verification flags

Ordered by risk. Three are still open and render inside the lesson as visibly marked teacher blocks, so nothing unverified can reach a student without being obvious. Search type: 'todo' in assets/data/ce-lesson-01-locker-ban.js to find them.

Flag 1, high

Bill naming and dates

Resolved in student text

The lesson says Senate Enrolled Act 78, signed March 2026, effective July 1, 2026. Precision to keep in mind:

  • Officially signed March 5, 2026. There was also a ceremonial signing on May 13, 2026, at Clark-Pleasant Community School Corporation in Whiteland, which is where most photos and governor quotes come from. Students who search will hit the May date first and think you got it wrong.
  • The enacted law is Senate Enrolled Act 78, amending IC 20-26-5-40.7. For citation, students should cite SEA 78 or the Indiana Code section, not "SB 78", which is the pre-passage bill. The Source Library entry says exactly this.
  • Vote: the Senate passed 36 to 12 in February 2026. Verify the House figure yourself before citing it.
  • Schools choose between a no-device policy and a storage policy. Mandatory exceptions: IEP, Section 504, documented medical condition with provider authorization, superintendent-authorized emergency, and language translation for multilingual learners. Teacher-directed instructional use on school-supplied or school-sanctioned devices is permitted through June 30, 2028, after which it is school-supplied only. That 2028 sunset is a good "this is not finished" detail for Step 8, and it is in the Source Library note.
Flag 2, high

ZCS policy specifics

Open, renders as a todo block

The policy detail in Step 1 comes from local news, not from the district: the ZCS Board of Trustees approved the updated policy July 13, 2026, choosing secured storage, applying during lunch and passing periods. The district source could not be loaded directly.

Pull the actual ZCS student handbook language and quote it verbatim in Step 1. It is your building, and the local primary source is the single best artifact in this lesson because students can verify it themselves.

Flag 3, medium

Framing tension, stated rationale against underlying cause

Resolved as option (b)

The official legislative record foregrounds focus, distraction and teacher enforcement burden, not mental health. Raatz talked about educational outcomes and enforcement. Braun's statements lead with focus and distraction, though the signing release does name mental health. The mental-health rationale is genuinely present, but it is not the loudest thing in the record.

Built as option (b), make the gap the lesson. Step 2 now ends with a section headed "the reason a law gives is not always the reason a law exists", which names the stated rationale and the underlying cause as different things and tells students to hold the gap open. Step 3 closes it with the evidence: SEA 78 and HEA 1408 from the same session, one about classroom order and one with nothing to do with classroom order at all. Contextualization in the skills framework is built on this move.

This also fixes the structural wobble where the chain switches tracks at Step 4. Steps 1 to 3 are policy lineage and Steps 4 to 6 are technology and mental health. Naming the distinction is the hinge that makes the switch legitimate rather than a bait and switch. To revert to option (a), delete the two blocks after the "reason a law gives" heading in Step 2 and the "two laws, weeks apart" section in Step 3.

Flag 4, medium

The Haidt excerpt

Open, renders as a todo block

The Anxious Generation is in copyright and no passage from it is reproduced anywhere in this repo. Step 4's primary-source slot is a marked placeholder. You select the excerpt under your normal classroom fair-use practice. A short passage where Haidt states the causal claim directly is ideal, because the two-column sort depends on the causal language being visible.

Freely usable alternatives and companions, all in the Source Library: Odgers's Nature review (March 2024), the governor's SEA 78 signing release, SEA 78 itself, and the Surgeon General's 2023 advisory on social media and youth mental health.

Step 4 already carries a recommendation block for the Haidt and Odgers pairing, which may serve you better than Haidt alone: it puts the contested causation in front of students at the evidence step instead of waiting until Step 7.

Flag 5, low

Numbers that drift

Partly open, one todo block
  • Teen smartphone ownership around 2012. Left unnumbered in Step 5 and marked as a todo block. Get the figures from Pew Research Center's teens and technology series and date-stamp them. Do not let a slide say 95 percent without a year and a source.
  • State counts. Trackers disagree because they count differently: Away for the Day reported 26 states with full bans as of May 2026; an Education Week analysis counted 22 bell-to-bell states in December 2025. Step 3 says "the majority of states" deliberately. If you want a hard number, pick one tracker, cite it and date it, and note that Illinois signed on July 28, 2026, so any number goes stale fast. That is a feature: it shows students this is live history.
  • SMART Schools reduction figures. Step 7 says roughly 40 minutes on phones and 30 on social media, from the university's release. The Lancet commentary cites about 30 minutes. Minor discrepancy, but be ready if a student cites it.
Flag 6, low but do not skip

Student wellbeing

Built in, action still needed

This lesson asks teenagers to analyze data about teenage mental-health decline, in a building where they can no longer reach a phone to text someone. Two things are already built:

  • Steps 4 and 7 are built on well-being, anxiety and depressive-symptom measures, not on suicide or self-harm rates. Haidt's argument leans on self-harm and suicide data, and there is a real argument about baselines and coding changes there, but raw youth suicide statistics in a ninth-grade slide deck is a different thing from an academic debate, and safe-messaging practice cuts against it. The well-being and anxiety measures carry the same argumentative weight without the risk.
  • A standing counselor line renders at the foot of all three coach bridges and on Steps 4 and 7. It lives in meta.counselorNote.

Still yours to do: make sure your MagicSchool configuration actually enforces the stop-and-refer rule below, and loop in your counseling team before you run this the first time. They may want to know it is coming.

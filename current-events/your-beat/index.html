Your Beat

Pick a story nobody assigned you, and spend the semester proving where it came from.

Everything we do together on the six course events, you do alone on one story of your own choosing. Any beat: politics, local, sports, music, games, tech, whatever you already follow. The only requirement is that it is real, it is current, and you can find sources on it.

Choose a format See the rubric
The brief

One story. Traced all the way back.

Your beat is not a report on a topic. It is a chain: this happened because of that, which happened because of this other thing, going backward until you reach something that surprises you. Each of the six course events comes with a checkpoint that moves your chain one link further back.

CP 1

Name your NOW

The event, the argument about it this month, and one suspected backward link.

CP 2

Set the scale

Where your story sits at the local, state, national, and global level.

CP 3

Build the chain

Four to six links, each one a cause of the link above it.

CP 4

Find the roots

At least two primary sources from the older end of your chain.

CP 5 & 6

Build and ship

Assemble the artifact in your chosen format, then submit it.

Choose one

Three formats. Same rubric under all of them.

You are graded on the tracing, not the medium. Pick the format you will actually finish.

Format A

Portfolio

A written and visual trace document. Strongest choice if you want to go deep on evidence and you write faster than you talk.

Format B

Video

A narrated walk backward through the origins. Six to ten minutes. Strongest choice if the chain is easier to show than to describe.

Format C

Mini-site

Your own small BeHistorical-style page with its own reverse timeline. Hardest and best. You end up building the thing you have been reading.

How it is graded

The rubric, in front of you from day one.

Five criteria, twenty points. The first four are the history. The fifth is the craft of whichever format you chose.

Criterion 4, Exceeds 3, Meets 2 or below, Not yet
Modern event named A specific, current, documented event, framed as a live argument with sides. A specific current event, clearly named and dated. A topic or a theme rather than an event.
Backward causal chain Every link is a real cause, and the last one genuinely surprises. Four or more links, each plausibly causing the one above it. Links are resemblances or coincidences, not causes.
Evidence and sources Primary sources at the roots, cited, with their limits acknowledged. Sources cited for each link, at least two of them primary. Uncited, or entirely secondary retellings.
Present-day significance Explains what the origin still determines about the event today. Explains why the trace matters now. Stops at the past without returning to the present.
Craft of the format The format actively helps the reader follow the chain. Clean, complete, and easy to follow. Unfinished, or the format fights the argument.

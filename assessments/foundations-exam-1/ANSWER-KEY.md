## Part A · Recall and applied recall (Questions 1-20)

### 1. A

**The Foundations reading argues that the key precondition for settled farming was a change in the climate. What did that change make possible?**

- A. Planting became a reasonable bet, because the weather stayed predictable  **← correct**
- B. People finally understood that seeds put in the ground grow into crops
- C. Wild plants and animals grew too scarce for foraging bands to live on
- D. Metal tools appeared that could clear forests and break up heavy soil

*Why:* The chapter frames planting as a bet that next year will resemble this one. The lurching climate of the last glacial made that bet bad; the steadiness of the Holocene made it reasonable.

*Source:* F1 §02 The Climate Turn

### 2. B

**Wheat became a domesticated crop mainly because harvesting with a sickle and replanting part of the harvest**

- A. forced early farmers to water and to weed their fields far more often
- B. favored the rare plants whose seeds stayed attached to the ripe stalk  **← correct**
- C. let farmers plant the very same field for several seasons in a row
- D. killed off the wild grasses that competed with wheat for good soil

*Why:* Cutting a stand and replanting part of it selects, unintentionally, for the non-shattering mutant. Over centuries the crop loses the ability to sow itself. That is domestication.

*Source:* F1 §03 What Domestication Actually Is

### 3. C

**According to the reading, the property of grain that mattered most for the rise of cities is that grain**

- A. grows more quickly than any other food available to early farmers
- B. contains more vitamins and protein than wild plants and wild game
- C. can be stored for years, counted by an official, and taken by force  **← correct**
- D. can be grown in almost every climate and soil type found on earth

*Why:* Storability is the hinge. Grain is durable, dense, divisible and countable, so it can feed non-farmers, be banked, be recorded, and be seized. Specialization, writing, taxation and hierarchy all sit on that.

*Source:* F1 §06 Surplus, and What It Really Does

### 4. D

**Skeletons from early farming populations in many regions show, compared with the foragers who came before them,**

- A. taller stature, stronger bones, and far fewer signs of infectious illness
- B. longer lives, better teeth, and much less evidence of hard physical labor
- C. almost no measurable difference in health, in height, or in physical wear
- D. shorter stature, more tooth decay, and more signs of childhood sickness  **← correct**

*Why:* Average stature declines, dental decay rises sharply on a starchy cereal diet, and enamel defects marking arrested growth become more common. The chapter calls this the price of the bargain.

*Source:* F1 §07 The Price of the Bargain

### 5. B

**Foundations 2 argues that the historically important question to ask about a belief system is**

- A. how many followers it gathered in the first century after it began
- B. what institutions it ran and what work those institutions actually did  **← correct**
- C. whether its central teachings can be shown to be true or to be false
- D. which of its rival traditions it managed to replace or to drive out

*Why:* The chapter asks four questions of every tradition: what it claims, what it ran, how it traveled, and who it put where. AP questions are almost always asking the second and third.

*Source:* F2 chapter deck and Section 01

### 6. A

**Buddhism spread widely across Asia in large part because donations to monasteries**

- A. earned merit for the giver, so lay wealth kept funding new monasteries  **← correct**
- B. were required by law in every kingdom that had adopted the Buddhist faith
- C. were collected by rulers who then built temples to display their own power
- D. replaced the taxes that merchants would otherwise have owed to their king

*Why:* Merit-making donation meant lay wealth flowed continuously into an institution that could be planted anywhere, which turned every oasis town and port into a possible node without any conquest.

*Source:* F2 §04 Buddhism

### 7. C

**The clearest reason Hinduism shaped enormous populations while converting almost nobody is that membership in it**

- A. required years of study in a monastery before a person could be admitted
- B. was granted only by rulers who had themselves been initiated by a priest
- C. came through birth and locality rather than through a personal decision  **← correct**
- D. depended on moving to a holy city and then living there for many years

*Why:* Born into versus joined is the single cleanest explanation of why one tradition stayed rooted in South Asia and the other crossed a continent.

*Source:* F2 §03 Hinduism, compared with §04

### 8. D

**The waqf endowment allowed madrasas and hospitals across the Islamic world to**

- A. charge students and patients a fee that religious law had fixed in advance
- B. admit any student at all, without an entrance requirement of any kind
- C. train the officials whom a ruler then appointed to govern his provinces
- D. keep operating through conquests, since their income was not the treasury  **← correct**

*Why:* A waqf is legally inalienable and funds an institution in perpetuity out of dedicated property revenue, so schools and hospitals survived the dynasties that ruled over them.

*Source:* F2 §07 Islam

### 9. B

**Persia held together the largest empire of its day mainly by**

- A. replacing local languages and religions with Persian ones everywhere
- B. leaving local religion, law, and elites in place under a royal governor  **← correct**
- C. moving conquered populations to distant provinces across the empire
- D. granting full Persian membership to the elites of conquered peoples

*Why:* Satrapies bought consent. A conquered people that keeps its gods, its language and its local rulers has far less reason to revolt, which made tolerance cheaper and more durable than terror.

*Source:* F3 §01 Achaemenid Persia

### 10. B

**Han officials were appointed by the center, paid a salary, moved between posts, and watched by a separate censorate. This design was meant to stop**

- A. officials from studying the Confucian classics before they took office
- B. administrative power from hardening into local hereditary family lines  **← correct**
- C. the emperor from having to approve every decision made in every county
- D. merchants from buying farmland in the commanderies near the capital

*Why:* Because posts were not hereditary, an official's son inherited status and opportunity but not the office, so the state did not slowly turn back into a collection of family fiefdoms.

*Source:* F3 §02 Han China

### 11. C

**The Mandate of Heaven was an unusual way to justify rule because it**

- A. claimed the emperor was a god himself, who could not be judged by anyone
- B. had to be renewed by a vote of the leading officials in every generation
- C. named a test the dynasty could visibly fail, which rebels could then cite  **← correct**
- D. applied only to the founder of a dynasty and to none of his successors

*Why:* The same doctrine that made the emperor the Son of Heaven handed any rebel a respectable argument. The Yellow Turbans in 184 CE claimed exactly that: Heaven had abandoned the Han.

*Source:* F3 §02 Han China, How it justified

### 12. A

**Athens filled its Council and its juries by lot rather than by election because Greek thinkers held that elections**

- A. reliably favored the famous, the wealthy, and the practiced speakers  **← correct**
- B. took far too long to organize in a city of that size and that wealth
- C. had been the method used by the kings whom Athens had already expelled
- D. could not be held safely during the long years Athens spent at war

*Why:* Election was understood as the aristocratic device and the lot as the democratic one, because the lot gives every citizen the same chance to govern and makes office a duty rather than a prize.

*Source:* F3 §03 The Greek Poleis

### 13. D

**Rome's distinctive answer to the problem of holding a diverse empire together was to**

- A. leave every conquered people entirely alone as long as tribute arrived
- B. settle Roman farmers on every piece of land the legions had conquered
- C. require each province to worship exactly the gods the city of Rome did
- D. offer the conquered a route into legal membership in the Roman state  **← correct**

*Why:* Auxiliary service, Latin status, municipal charters, and finally the grant of 212 CE gave provincials a personal stake in the empire's survival instead of a grievance against it.

*Source:* F3 §04 Rome

### 14. B

**To say that the Silk Roads worked as a relay means that**

- A. caravans traveled the whole route together from China as far as Rome
- B. each merchant worked one stage he knew and sold on to the next man  **← correct**
- C. goods moved only while a single empire controlled the entire route
- D. merchants were paid by the state rather than by their own customers

*Why:* One merchant traveling four thousand kilometers and back was slower, more dangerous and less profitable for everyone than a chain of specialists each working a segment he knew.

*Source:* F4 §01 The Silk Roads

### 15. D

**Because prices multiplied at every transfer along the Silk Roads, the goods carried overland were mostly**

- A. heavy staples such as grain, timber, and building stone from the east
- B. live animals able to walk the route without needing to be carried
- C. goods that nobody living along the route could produce for themselves
- D. light items worth a great deal for their weight, such as silk and gems  **← correct**

*Why:* A high value-to-weight ratio is what survives a chain of markups: silk, gems, spices, glass, medicines. Nobody hauled grain to Rome overland.

*Source:* F4 §01 The Silk Roads

### 16. C

**The monsoon shaped Indian Ocean trade above all because it**

- A. blew hard enough to push a ship from Africa to China without a stop
- B. made the ocean far too dangerous to cross for most months of the year
- C. reversed on a schedule, so a merchant waited months to sail back home  **← correct**
- D. let ships sail in any direction they chose at any time of the year

*Why:* A voyage out and back took the better part of a year, so merchants overwintered in foreign ports. The wind built the permanent diaspora communities, the conversions and the creole languages.

*Source:* F4 §02 The Indian Ocean

### 17. A

**The camel and its saddle changed trans-Saharan trade chiefly by**

- A. lowering the cost of a crossing enough to make regular trade worth doing  **← correct**
- B. making the very first crossing of the Sahara possible for human beings
- C. allowing a caravan to cross without stopping at any well or oasis at all
- D. removing the need for merchants to hire guides who knew the desert wells

*Why:* The Sahara did not shrink. The chapter is explicit that the desert had been crossed before; what changed was the arithmetic, so a marginal journey became an ordinary commercial operation.

*Source:* F4 §03 The Trans-Saharan Routes

### 18. C

**The reading insists that Song China at c.1200 has to be described as both**

- A. the largest empire on earth and the most religiously tolerant of states
- B. the poorest of the major regions and the most inventive technologically
- C. commercially the most advanced economy and militarily very vulnerable  **← correct**
- D. united under a single ruler and completely closed to all foreign trade

*Why:* The most commercially advanced state in the world was paying tribute to its northern neighbors to avoid war. A student who remembers only the prosperity cannot explain what happens next.

*Source:* F5 §01 Song China

### 19. A

**Dar al-Islam at c.1200 is the chapter's best evidence that a world can be**

- A. politically divided and still connected by law, language, and learning  **← correct**
- B. ruled by one caliph whose orders reached from Iberia to Central Asia
- C. completely cut off from trade and still produce great works of science
- D. united by a single language and still unable to move goods or ideas

*Why:* There is no single ruler and has not been for centuries, and it is nevertheless one connected world. Political unity and cultural coherence are different things.

*Source:* F5 §02 Dar al-Islam

### 20. D

**The most consequential result of the Americas' separation from Afro-Eurasia was that**

- A. they never developed cities, monumental building, or long-distance trade
- B. they had no farming at all and lived entirely by hunting and gathering
- C. their societies stayed essentially unchanged for the centuries before 1492
- D. their populations had no exposure to the epidemic diseases of Afro-Eurasia  **← correct**

*Why:* Afro-Eurasian epidemic diseases emerged largely from long contact with domesticated herd animals the Americas did not have, so there was no exposure and no acquired resistance.

*Source:* F5 §05 The Americas

### 36. B

**Which sentence does the work of contextualization, rather than simply describing?**

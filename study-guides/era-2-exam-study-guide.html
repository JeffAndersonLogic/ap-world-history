How to use this guide: Do not study this era as a list of names. The exam emphasizes cause and effect, continuity and change, comparison, state building, and the effects of exchange networks. For every topic below, be able to explain why a development mattered and connect evidence to a broader historical claim.
BeHistorical Study System

Study for explanation, not recognition.

01 · KNOW IT

Name the evidence.

For every topic, be able to identify at least 2–3 specific historical examples without looking them up.

02 · EXPLAIN IT

Connect evidence to process.

Be able to explain how the evidence demonstrates state building, exchange, diffusion, continuity/change, or causation.

03 · COMPARE IT

Know what makes it different.

Strong answers identify a meaningful similarity or difference and then explain why that difference existed.

When You See a Stimulus
WHO / WHAT?Identify the person, society, institution, map, image, or network represented.
WHEN / WHERE?Use the time period and geography to eliminate answers that do not fit the historical context.
WHAT PROCESS?Ask whether the evidence is about state building, exchange, diffusion, belief, economy, or environment.
SO WHAT?Connect the evidence to cause/effect, continuity/change, or comparison before choosing an answer.
Common Traps
Mongols & trade
The Mongols intensified and protected existing Silk Road networks; they did not create them.
Monsoons
Monsoon winds are an environmental pattern that sailors learned to exploit, not a technology.
Bills of exchange
Credit instruments reduced the need to move large quantities of coin over long distances.
Diasporic communities
These were merchant communities living outside their homelands, not simply travelers passing through.
Connectivity
Expanded exchange could produce benefits such as crop diffusion and costs such as epidemic disease.
State extraction
Mexica tribute and Inca mit'a both extracted resources, but one emphasized tribute and the other state labor obligations.
Song vs. Europe
Both were hierarchical, but Song China was much more centralized through imperial bureaucracy than western Europe.
Religion & power
Belief systems shaped culture, but they could also help rulers justify authority and build political legitimacy.

Unit 1 · The Global Tapestry

Topic 1.1

Developments in East Asia — Song China

Know
  • Confucian civil service examinations and the scholar-official bureaucracy
  • Confucianism and Neo-Confucianism as sources of cultural continuity
  • Champa rice, the Grand Canal, paper money, expanding cities, specialized manufacturing
  • Chinese cultural influence on neighboring East Asian societies
Be able to explain
  • How the Song used bureaucracy and Confucian ideas to maintain rule
  • How agricultural and commercial innovations supported population growth and urbanization
  • How Chinese traditions spread while being adapted locally
  • Why Song China was more politically centralized than western Europe
Evidence anchors: civil service exams · Neo-Confucianism · Champa rice · Grand Canal · paper money
Topic 1.2

Developments in Dar al-Islam

Know
  • Fragmentation of Abbasid political authority
  • Rise of regional Islamic states and Turkic military elites
  • Sufi teachers, Muslim merchants, pilgrims, scholars, and legal traditions
  • Intellectual exchange in mathematics, medicine, philosophy, geography, and related fields
Be able to explain
  • How political decentralization could coexist with cultural and religious continuity
  • How Islam spread through networks as well as political expansion
  • Why centers of learning remained important after Baghdad's decline
  • How intellectual exchange crossed political and cultural boundaries
Topic 1.3

South & Southeast Asia

Know
  • Bhakti traditions and the wider pattern of devotional religion
  • Religious patronage as a source of political legitimacy
  • Khmer, Majapahit, and other South/Southeast Asian state-building patterns
  • Strategic ports, sea lanes, taxation of trade, and maritime power
Be able to explain
  • How rulers connected political authority to sacred traditions
  • How temples, monasteries, and religious institutions supported state power
  • Why maritime states benefited from controlling ports and sea routes
  • How devotional movements could broaden religious participation
Topic 1.4

State Building in the Americas

Know
  • Inca roads and relay stations
  • State storehouses and record keeping
  • Mit'a labor obligations
  • Mexica tribute system
Be able to explain
  • How the Inca administered a large and difficult territory
  • How labor obligations supported imperial administration
  • The key difference between Mexica tribute extraction and Inca labor mobilization
Topic 1.5

State Building in Africa

Know
  • Mali and trans-Saharan gold commerce
  • Great Zimbabwe and links between interior resources and Indian Ocean ports
  • Ethiopian Christianity and dynastic legitimacy
  • Trade, taxation, monumental building, and political authority
Be able to explain
  • How rulers converted commercial wealth into state power
  • How Ethiopia used religion to reinforce political legitimacy
  • How Mali and Great Zimbabwe illustrate different African state-building strategies
Topic 1.6

Developments in Europe

Know
  • Feudal relationships and political decentralization
  • Christian Church institutions and their social influence
  • Manorialism, serfdom, rents, labor services, and customary dues
  • Local lords as important political and economic authorities
Be able to explain
  • Why political authority was fragmented in much of western Europe
  • How Christianity linked communities across decentralized territories
  • How agricultural production connected to local hierarchy through manorialism and serfdom
Topic 1.7

Comparison in the Period c. 1200–1450

You should be ready to compare
  • Song China vs. western Europe: centralized imperial bureaucracy vs. more fragmented political authority
  • Confucianism vs. Christianity: different belief systems that could reinforce political and social order
  • Inca vs. Great Zimbabwe: both mobilized resources for state power, but through different institutions and economic systems
  • Mexica vs. Inca: tribute extraction vs. large-scale state labor obligations

Unit 2 · Networks of Exchange

Topic 2.1

The Silk Roads

Know
  • Caravanserai
  • Credit, bills of exchange, and commercial practices
  • Kashgar and Samarkand as major commercial cities
  • Luxury goods such as silk and porcelain
Be able to explain
  • How infrastructure reduced the logistical risks of overland trade
  • Why credit reduced the need to transport large quantities of coin
  • Why high-value, low-bulk luxury goods fit overland transportation
Topic 2.2

The Mongol Empire: Conquest, Administration & Connectivity

Know
  • Genghis Khan's unification of the Mongol steppe and merit-based military organization
  • Cavalry mobility, mounted archery, feigned retreats, and adapted siege technologies
  • Division of the empire into khanates and use of local administrators and specialists
  • Religious tolerance as a strategy for governing diverse populations
  • Yam relay stations, route protection, and the Pax Mongolica
  • Black Death as an unintended consequence of intensified connectivity
Be able to explain
  • How military organization and borrowed technologies helped the Mongols build a vast land empire
  • How the Mongols administered culturally and religiously diverse territories
  • How the Yam system supported both government communication and long-distance movement
  • How Mongol political conditions intensified trade without creating the Silk Roads
  • Why increased connectivity produced both commercial benefits and biological costs
  • Continuity: routes already existed; change: movement and protection intensified
Evidence anchors: Genghis Khan · mounted cavalry · siege engineers · khanates · religious tolerance · Yam system · Pax Mongolica
Topic 2.3

Exchange in the Indian Ocean

Know
  • Seasonal monsoon winds
  • Lateen sails, improved ship designs, compass, and astrolabe
  • Diasporic merchant communities
  • Swahili city-states
Be able to explain
  • How environmental knowledge shaped sailing schedules
  • How maritime technologies supported long-distance commerce
  • How merchant communities produced cultural interaction and syncretism
  • How trade could contribute directly to state formation
Topic 2.4

Trans-Saharan Trade Routes

Know
  • Camel saddle and desert caravan travel
  • Mali, gold, taxation, and trans-Saharan commerce
  • Mansa Musa and Mali's international visibility
  • Timbuktu, Islamic scholarship, and manuscript culture
Be able to explain
  • How transportation technology expanded desert commerce
  • How rulers converted control of trade into revenue and political power
  • How commerce also supported cultural and intellectual exchange
Topic 2.5

Cultural Consequences of Connectivity

Know
  • Cultural syncretism
  • Swahili culture as a blend of Bantu and Islamic/commercial influences
  • Technological diffusion across Afro-Eurasia
  • Gunpowder's westward movement from China
Be able to explain
  • How trade networks moved ideas and technologies as well as goods
  • How local traditions could combine with incoming cultural influences
  • Why diffusion usually involved adaptation rather than simple copying
Topic 2.6

Environmental Consequences of Connectivity

Know
  • Movement of crops into new environments, including bananas into parts of Africa and new rice varieties across Asia
  • Movement of citrus and other food crops through Afro-Eurasian exchange networks
  • Movement of pathogens and disease vectors along commercial and imperial routes
  • Black Death and fourteenth-century demographic decline across Afro-Eurasia
Be able to explain
  • How crop diffusion could change diets, agricultural productivity, settlement, and population patterns
  • The causal chain: increased exchange → movement of disease vectors/pathogens → epidemics → population decline
  • Why the same networks that spread useful crops, technologies, and goods could also spread disease
  • Why environmental consequences are evidence that connectivity had mixed rather than uniformly positive effects
Evidence anchors: bananas · rice varieties · citrus · pathogens · trade routes · Black Death
Topic 2.7

Comparison of Economic Exchange

Big idea

The Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, and trans-Saharan networks all expanded because technologies, commercial practices, and supportive political conditions made exchange more reliable. Geography shaped how each network worked.

Must-Master Cross-Topic Comparisons

ComparisonWhat you should be able to say
Song China vs. western EuropeSong China used a centralized scholar-official bureaucracy; western Europe was more politically fragmented through feudal relationships and local elites.
Mexica vs. IncaBoth extracted resources from subject populations, but the Mexica relied more heavily on tribute while the Inca mobilized large-scale labor obligations.
Mali vs. Great ZimbabweBoth benefited from long-distance trade; Mali was tied to trans-Saharan commerce, while Great Zimbabwe linked interior resources to Indian Ocean routes.
Silk Roads vs. Indian OceanBoth carried goods and culture, but maritime shipping moved larger bulk cargo more efficiently while overland trade favored high-value goods.
Indian Ocean vs. trans-SaharanBoth depended on specialized technologies and environmental knowledge: monsoon sailing at sea and camel-caravan systems across the desert.
All three exchange networksAll expanded through improved transport, commercial practices, and political support, but geography shaped travel methods, goods, and patterns of interaction.

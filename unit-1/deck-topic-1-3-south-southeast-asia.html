AP World History · Unit 1 · Topic 1.3

Developments in South and Southeast Asia

States, Belief Systems, and Trade · c. 1200 to c. 1450

BeHistorical

The Question

How do you build a state out of belief and geography?

  • Belief shapes society
  • Belief builds states
  • Geography decides which states win

Where in the World

One region, three geographies

  • South Asia: Islamic north, Hindu south
  • Mainland: rivers, rice, irrigation
  • Maritime: straits, ports, ships

Write this down

Mainland states grew from rice. Island states grew from trade.

Religions of the Region

Three traditions, one region

Oldest, deepest

Hinduism

Caste and temple kingship

Monastic, mobile

Buddhism

Monasteries along the trade routes

Newest, arriving

Islam

Conquest in the north, commerce in the south

Write this down

Not sealed boxes. Rulers and merchants moved between all three.

Before Part One

Hinduism and the social order

  • Brahman (cosmic force) and dharma (sacred duty)
  • Four varnas, born in, no moving out
  • Karma across lifetimes enforces the system

Write this down

Caste was cosmic, not social. Your duty was to the universe.

The Big Picture

Three religions, three roads

  • Hinduism: merchants and royal courts
  • Buddhism: monks and sea routes
  • Islam: conquest north, commerce south

Write this down

Same ports, same sea lanes. Three religions, one crossroads.

Part One

Belief reshapes society

Two movements, five hundred miles apart, doing the same thing: moving religion out of the hands of an institution and into the hands of a person.

Movement One · South India

Bhakti: devotion over hierarchy

  • Devotion, not ritual or caste
  • Led by low-caste poets and women
  • Reform from inside Hinduism

Write this down

Hinduism did not fracture. It opened a closed door.

Movement Two · North India and beyond

Sufism: Islam through connection

  • Mystics, poets, teachers
  • Adapted to local custom
  • Delhi Sultanate: law plus accommodation

Write this down

Islam came through two doors: an army, and a teacher.

Comparison

Two movements, one shape

 BhaktiSufism
TraditionHinduIslamic

Part Two

Belief builds states

A ruler cannot stand behind every farmer with a spear. So he needs subjects who believe that obeying him is the right thing to do.

The Pattern

How a temple becomes political power

Step 1

Patronize

Fund the temple

Step 2

Employ

Priests, artisans, land

Step 3

Gather

Pilgrims, tribute, trade

Step 4

Obey

Rule looks like the natural order

Write this down

Never stop at “religion legitimized power”. Name the mechanism.

Case One · South Asia

Vijayanagara: the temple as anchor

  • Founded 1336, Hindu Deccan
  • Temple culture anchors authority
  • Hampi: among the world's richest cities

Discuss, do not write

Patronage, pilgrims, markets, revenue, army.

Case Two · Mainland Southeast Asia

Angkor: the god-king and the water

The claim

Devaraja

The god-king. Temple as a model of the universe.

The engine

Water and rice

Reservoirs, dry-season crop, surplus labor

The proof

Angkor Wat

Largest religious structure on earth

Write this down

Control the water, control the rice, control the labor.

Part Three

Geography builds states

Take away the farmland and the army. What is left to build an empire on? The wind, and a narrow place in the water.

Case Three · Maritime Southeast Asia

Power without an army

  • Srivijaya taxed the strait
  • Majapahit ruled by sea, not by soil
  • Their ships carried Buddhism and Hinduism

Write this down

Hold a chokepoint, and you need not hold the land.

Why one narrow strait mattered so much

Dar al-Islam: One Civilization, Many States

c. 1200–c. 1450
GOV · CDI · TEC

Essential understanding: Political power fragmented after the Abbasids, but shared belief, trade, law, travel, and scholarship kept Dar al-Islam connected across Afro-Eurasia.

Layer 1 · Governance

Power After the Abbasids

What changed?

The Abbasid Caliphate fragmented. New Islamic political entities emerged, many led by Turkic military elites.

How did it work?

Regional sultans controlled armies, taxes, and officials while Islamic institutions supplied law and legitimacy.

Evidence to know

  • Seljuk Empire
  • Mamluk Sultanate of Egypt
  • Delhi Sultanates

Effect: Political diversity increased, but Islamic government and culture continued in multiple regional centers.

Layer 2 · Culture

Rule Is Not Conversion

What changed?

Muslim rule expanded through warfare, while Islam spread farther through non-state networks.

How did it spread?

Merchants, missionaries, and Sufis carried Islam through ports, trade routes, teaching, and personal relationships.

Evidence to know

  • Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan trade
  • Sufi adaptation to local cultures
  • Hindu majority under Muslim rule in Delhi

Effect: Islam connected distant communities, but conversion was gradual and local cultures persisted.

Layer 3 · Innovation

Knowledge Across Borders

What made it possible?

Rulers and patrons supported scholars, libraries, hospitals, observatories, translation, and travel.

What did scholars do?

They preserved, commented on, tested, and extended Greek, Persian, and Indian knowledge.

Evidence to know

  • Nasir al-Din al-Tusi: mathematics and astronomy
  • A’ishah al-Ba’uniyyah: literature
  • Medicine, House of Wisdom, and exchange in Spain

Effect: Innovations and ideas circulated across Afro-Eurasia and survived the destruction of Baghdad in 1258.

Keep These Distinctions Straight

  • Dar al-Islam was not one empire.
  • Muslim rule did not equal mass conversion.
  • 1258 ended Abbasid rule in Iraq, not Islamic learning.
  • The CED asks for both innovation and transfer, not preservation alone.
